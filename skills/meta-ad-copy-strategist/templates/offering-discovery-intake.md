# Offering discovery intake — product / service / subscription / package

**Use adaptively.** Do not require a long questionnaire if the product page and supplied files already answer it. Gather only relevant missing information; label blanks.

## 1. Context and identity
- Brand and any approved brand voice/messaging guide:
- Exact offering and type: physical SKU / collection / digital product / software / subscription / service / bundle
- Website, specific product URL, listing, catalog ID or service proposal:
- Market and geography:
- Lifecycle stage: research / prelaunch / established / declining / repositioning
- User's commercial goal and requested output:
- Who can approve claims and spend?

## 2. What is actually received?
- Core deliverables, features, specifications, quantity, variants or scope:
- Inclusion / optional extras / exclusions:
- Prerequisites, compatibility, restrictions and realistic limits:
- For services: who delivers, process, client effort, timeline, capacity and handoffs:
- How it's made/performed (only what can be verified):
- Available photos, demo, documents, genuine reviews and rights:
- Support, changes, upgrades, maintenance or onboarding:

## 3. Buying situations and alternatives
- Buyers, users, recipients, payers, approvers and decision influencers:
- Why does purchase come up, and how urgent is the occasion?
- Main desired outcomes, aesthetic/emotional/social value, pain relief:
- Current alternatives, including no purchase / manual workaround:
- What pushes buyers toward this offering / attracts them?
- What keeps them with their current solution?
- Buyer questions, complaints, past objections and postpurchase surprises:
- What are genuine reasons the product/service may be the wrong fit?

## 4. Offer, pricing and evidence
- Actual price/options, discounts and terms:
- Shipping, tax, implementation, renewal, time and effort costs:
- Cost of goods or delivery, variable fees, returns, capacity and allowable acquisition economics:
- Genuine warranties, exchanges, refunds, trials, service-level terms:
- Claims requiring substantiation: production method, origin, efficacy, environmental/health/financial claims, comparisons:
- Evidence and approval links for each claim:

## 5. Marketing readiness
- Category and competitors considered by actual buyers:
- Existing PDP/landing page, visuals, story, FAQs and purchase path:
- Existing channels, sales sources, recent ad/campaign performance:
- Target channels or constraints:
- Budget, inventory/capacity, business priorities:
- Primary hypotheses to test and how success will be measured:

## Output checklist
- [ ] Offering definition and scope
- [ ] Brand context and voice status
- [ ] Buyer-by-job and occasion matrix
- [ ] Real alternatives and category parity/difference
- [ ] Feature → benefit → proof → tradeoff map
- [ ] Purchase motivation, objection, risk and assurance map
- [ ] Price, total cost and economics review
- [ ] Claim ledger and creative asset gaps
- [ ] Prioritized value proposition and positioning
- [ ] Reusable bullets/headlines/descriptions/Meta ad units
- [ ] Marketing channel, creative, landing-page and test plan
- [ ] Evidence vs hypothesis explicitly distinguished
