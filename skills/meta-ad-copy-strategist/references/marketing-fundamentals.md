# Marketing fundamentals: diagnosis before copy

**Use when:** a user asks for a strategy, a new campaign, product positioning, brand direction, or improvements to an underperforming ad. Do not make a user seeking a light copy edit complete a lengthy strategy exercise.

## Marketing is a system, not just promotion

Marketing includes choosing *whom to serve*, understanding the value to create and deliver, making it accessible, and communicating it. Separate these decisions:

| Decision | Question | Diagnostic failure | Response other than copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market and demand | Is a category occasion or desire present? | No plausible buying situation | Research demand and occasions |
| Segmentation and target | Which buyers value this proposition most? | Generic appeal to everyone | Segment by needs and circumstances |
| Product | Does the offering actually create the desired benefit? | Missing capability, poor quality | Product change, SKU selection |
| Positioning | Compared with what, why choose us? | No clear value relative to alternatives | Reposition or improve differentiation |
| Brand | Will people recognize or remember who made the promise? | Good ad but no brand recognition | Consistent distinctive assets, category cues |
| Price/offer | Is value attractive relative to total sacrifice? | Price, terms, risk or friction exceed value | Adjust price, bundle, guarantee, conditions |
| Place/distribution | Can the customer find and buy it? | Stock, delivery, placement, checkout blockers | Fix availability and UX |
| Promotion/creative | Is value communicated clearly to the right people? | Message mismatch, wrong proof, bad creative | Better creative and copy |
| Measurement | Does the marketing create profitable incremental outcomes? | Optimizing clicks or correlated sales | Better outcome data and experimental design |

This adapts conventional segmentation-targeting-positioning (STP) and marketing-mix thinking (product, price, place, promotion) into a practical diagnosis. It is **not** a claim that every business needs a formal framework document.

## Diagnostic routing

1. Identify whether the request is **marketing strategy**, **brand-building**, **positioning**, **conversion activation**, **copy editing**, or **performance diagnosis**.
2. Determine the business objective and appropriate outcome metric (revenue/margin, leads, memory/recognition, repeat use). Do not assume every campaign should maximize short-term ROAS.
3. Collect the minimum facts: offering, alternatives, customer situation, price/terms, available proof, destination, creative assets, brand identifiers, objective, previous results.
4. Write **one or more competing explanations** for the opportunity or problem. Label observed vs hypothesized causes.
5. Locate the binding constraint. Weak copy? Unconvincing offer? Insufficient differentiation? Unfamiliar brand? Sizing uncertainty? Checkout failure? Sometimes several.
6. Recommend the cheapest *informative* next step. A product-page fix, a customer interview, a controlled ad concept test, or a new copy variant are all legitimate outcomes.

### Example: clicks but no purchases

Do not immediately replace the headline. Investigate traffic relevance, price relative to alternatives, product images, review credibility, SKU options, delivery cost, checkout usability, conversion tracking and sample size. If the product page does not state ring size, a headline cannot resolve the underlying information gap reliably.

## Theories are lenses, not universal laws

- **JTBD** explains progress customers want in concrete situations; it does not imply every purchase solves a painful problem.
- **Dunford positioning** clarifies choice relative to customer-perceived alternatives; it should not be confused with writing a slogan.
- **Keller brand equity** analyzes memory associations and consumer response; **Ehrenberg-Bass** emphasizes being easy to think of and buy. Use both without forcing agreement on every brand-growth claim.
- **Cialdini, ELM and signaling** suggest mechanisms influencing perception; none grants license to invent authority, urgency or proof.
- **Binet and Field** examine short- and long-term effects; aggregate budget ratios are not guaranteed prescriptions for individual accounts.

## Output: strategic diagnosis

For larger tasks, report:

- **Business and campaign objective** and constraints;
- **Buyer situations** and possible value sought;
- **Alternative set** and category expectations;
- **Product/brand value and credible evidence**;
- **Likely blockers** split into communication, offer, product, delivery and measurement;
- **Recommended lever** and reason;
- **Evidence confidence and what would disconfirm the diagnosis**;
- **Next experiment or intervention**, not just proposed wording.
