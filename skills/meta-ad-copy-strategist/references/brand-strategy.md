# Brand strategy: memory, meaning, recognition and relevance

**Use when:** brand identity, memorability, trust, top-of-funnel advertising, creative consistency or brand-vs-performance strategy could change the answer.

**For brand discovery, voice, perspective and reusable copy assets:** run [Workflow 3A](../workflows/03a-brand-discovery-and-messaging.md), then read [the brand voice/copy system](brand-voice-and-copy-system.md) and complete [the messaging library](../templates/brand-messaging-library.md).

## A brand exists partly in customer memory

Kevin Lane Keller's customer-based brand equity model distinguishes brand awareness from brand image/associations and asks how brand knowledge changes responses to the same marketing. Ehrenberg-Bass research emphasizes *mental availability* (being thought of in purchase situations) and *physical availability* (being easy to find/buy). These are different but useful perspectives, not mutually exclusive copywriting formulas.

### Keep three concepts separate

| Concept | Customer question | Typical levers | How to evaluate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distinctiveness** | Can I recognize whose ad/product this is? | Name, packaging, color, typography, logo, recurring motif, consistent product presentation | Correct attribution, recognition of assets |
| **Differentiation** | Why prefer this over another option? | Features, service, style, price, performance, experience | Preferences and actual comparisons |
| **Meaning / associations** | What does this brand evoke or stand for? | Consistent experiences, cues, storytelling, product delivery | Association research and authentic buyer descriptions |

Do **not** assume being visually unusual makes a product superior, or that a difference matters merely because it exists. Also, a brand can be quite recognizable without being strongly differentiated.

## Category entry points (CEPs)

CEPs are cues—situations, goals, contexts or occasions—that activate thoughts about a category. Illustrative jewelry CEPs: an upcoming birthday, wanting something to wear to an event, a small gift, a style refresh. **These are candidate cues, not proven consumer frequencies.**

For each cue ask:

1. Does this purchase situation actually recur and matter for the category?
2. Is the brand believable and relevant in that context?
3. Can the ad connect the brand and cue in the *same moment* (image, text, voice, logo or packaging)?
4. Which recognizable brand elements will be present, legible and repeated over time?
5. Is there a route to purchase when the cue arises (SKU availability, price point, retailer, checkout)?

Avoid randomizing every visual, tagline and tone in pursuit of 'creative variety'. Vary **strategic angles** while protecting stable, recognizable assets.

## Brand code / identity inventory

Document where available:

- **Name and product category:** what must a viewer remember, and how quickly can they infer what is sold?
- **Distinctive visual assets:** logo, palette, typography, shapes, packaging, photographic language, product silhouette.
- **Verbal assets:** recognizable naming patterns, product vocabulary, voice, recurring phrases. Do not manufacture a slogan just to fill a box.
- **Values and personality:** only if demonstrably expressed through product, service or communication; avoid inventing a moral mission.
- **Known associations:** from real customer research, not the brand owner's aspirations alone.
- **Consistency constraints:** required asset visibility, exclusions and non-negotiable tone.

## Brand vs direct response: select an objective first

| Objective | Message emphasis | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate sales | Relevant offer, clarity, benefits, risk reduction, next step | Contribution, purchases, incrementality, CPA, ROAS with margin context |
| Brand building | Recognizable brand plus memorable, relevant CEP and emotions/meaning | Reach of category buyers, correctly attributed recall/recognition, associations, eventual incremental growth |
| Hybrid | Memorable brand presence plus easy next action | Both groups of metrics, measured in their appropriate horizons |

Avoid treating a low short-term ROAS as conclusive evidence of zero brand effect; equally, do not use hypothetical long-term brand lift to excuse a campaign with no credible measurement.

## Questions for an ad audit

- If the logo disappeared, could customers still recognize the brand? If not, is that okay for the creative objective?
- Would someone who liked the ad know which brand to search for later?
- Is the brand embedded in the buying situation or tacked on as a tiny watermark?
- Do several ads preserve enough common assets while exploring different purchase motivations?
- Does the actual product and customer experience confirm the brand's promise?

## Illustrative application

A collection of vintage-inspired rings might have a recognizable close-up photography style and repeating packaging motif. For a gifting ad, show a *real* product and any *verified* packaging; pair the visual with the brand name and gift occasion. The benefit might be thoughtful selection. Do not claim heirloom craftsmanship, years in business, or special gift presentation without proof.
