# Instagram/Meta communication idioms and brand voice

**Idiom** means recognizable platform storytelling forms *and* natural expressions used by particular communities. These aren't magic words. Their purpose is to reduce decoding effort and communicate something valuable in a way that fits the brand, buyer, and product.

## Separate the four languages in an ad

1. **Visual syntax:** What the clip/shot means before anyone reads—try-on, close-up, demo, direct address, reveal, evidence.
2. **On-screen overlay:** Often the only read text in Reels/Stories; communicates the main point in very few words. Must be visible in safe zone.
3. **Voiceover/captions:** Natural explanation, product experience or story; subtitles should match what is said.
4. **Meta fields:** Primary text (feed caption-like), headline and description (sometimes omitted or remixed), CTA and destination. Do not copy every sentence into all four channels.

## Useful platform-native forms, with guardrails

| Native grammar | Authentic use | Do not do |
|---|---|---|
| `POV:` | A genuinely recognizable customer point of view or purchase occasion | Invent a customer's lived experience or imply personal attributes |
| `GRWM` / 'get ready with me' | Real styling/selection sequence, relevant to category | Pretend there's a creator using an item when there isn't |
| `Unboxing` | Actual packaging/contents and reveal | Show premium packaging that isn't supplied |
| `Try-on` | Real fit/scale and styled context | Distort dimensions/material, conceal fit issues |
| `3 ways to...` | Three real uses/looks/options visually demonstrated | State hypothetical uses as validated suitability |
| `A closer look` / `the details` | Macro detail that adds new information | Generic filler video with no visible detail |
| `Which would you choose?` | Genuine alternatives, encourages exploration | Rely on vanity engagement without sales intent |
| `ASMR` / satisfying sound | Genuine product-associated sound, if appropriate | Fabricated product material acoustics used as proof |
| Q&A / objection reply | Answer actual sizing, timing, construction questions | Invent customer questions or claim universal satisfaction |
| Creator story | Real creator with authorization, honest perspective | Fake UGC, impersonation, undisclosed payment |
| Carousel 'swipe for...' | Ordered visual narrative that pays off on next cards | Hide core product identity until last card |

## Natural diction: category-first and evidence-first

- Replace interchangeable copy: `Elevate your everyday` → `Vintage-inspired detailing, shown up close.`
- Replace empty hype: `Our best ever!` → state a verified new feature or omit.
- Replace urgency theater: `Hurry! Ending tonight!` → use real promo start/end only when true and material.
- Replace awkward high-pressure questions: `Are you tired of boring jewelry?` → show the product and a buyer-relevant occasion without presuming a personal deficit.
- Replace faux intimacy: `Girlies, you're obsessed` → use brand's actual audience language only when documented and fitting.
- Prefer concrete nouns, active verbs, and speech-shaped sentences; don't always force short choppy fragments or generic hooks.

## Tone calibration matrix

Record: `audience register | product category | brand personality | established customer expressions | approved idioms | prohibited clichés | localization`. Example:

- **Quiet heritage**: restrained, concrete materials/style words; avoid excessive emojis and breathless urgency.
- **Playful gifting**: warm, situational, light; avoid pretending to know recipients or birthdays.
- **Technical/functional**: demonstration, comparisons, specifications; avoid unsupported superlatives.
- **Creator-direct**: first-person only when an actual authorized person is speaking about their own real experience.

## Do / don't communication rules

**Do:** one main message; show the fact; keep CTA specific; use the exact product naming; let the visual and caption contribute different information; maintain brand attribution; use authentic customer language where available.

**Don't:** overuse emojis/hashtags; invent slang as a substitute for research; use unsupported transformation or scarcity claims; overload text; assume all viewers see captions or hear audio; put critical conditions in a hidden description; create fake DM/chat/notification UI to simulate a consumer endorsement.

## Idiom decision gate

Before adopting a trend or format ask: (1) Would this buyer recognize it? (2) Does it illustrate a real advantage? (3) Is it compatible with brand voice? (4) Are music, footage, talent and usage rights cleared? (5) Will the creative still make sense when trend expires? If any answer is no, choose a clearer execution.
