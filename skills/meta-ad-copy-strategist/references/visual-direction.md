# Visual direction and art direction for paid social

**Use when:** writing shot lists, generating artwork or creative briefs, selecting product photography, adapting assets, or critiquing ads. A visual should *demonstrate* a commercially meaningful idea, not decorate copy.

## The direction hierarchy

1. **Hero:** What must the viewer see in the first glance—product, usage, person's expression, transformation, offer, or category cue?
2. **Promise:** What one buyer-relevant proposition can the visual communicate with minimal reading?
3. **Proof:** How is the promise visibly credible (detail/scale/demonstration/materials/real review with permission)?
4. **Brand:** What makes it attributable (recognizable package, typography, recurring environment or product treatment, name/logo)?
5. **Action:** What is the simplest next step consistent with the landing page?

### One-frame comprehension test

At mobile thumbnail scale: can a viewer identify the category, object, key detail, and brand? If not, remove elements or change crop. Focal-point rules are *design heuristics*, not proof of better conversion.

## Art-direction language: describe decisions, not moods

Bad: `make it premium, aesthetic, engaging, viral`.

Useful: `Top-down 4:5 macro of the actual ring on a neutral warm-gray surface. One product, no props crossing its silhouette. Soft side light reveals engraved detail without whitening the metal. Clear 20% negative space above ring for a six-word overlay. Include a small, legible wordmark in its established type treatment. Do not alter metal color, stone count or size.`

Art-direction parameters:

| Dimension | Specify | Risk to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Subject & truth | SKU, variant, dimensions, materials, color | AI additions, inaccurate scale, misleading retouching |
| Composition | hero size, placement, negative space, crop, eye path | competing focal points, tiny product |
| Camera/shot | macro, detail, POV, try-on, tabletop, wide contextual | visual ambiguity about size/use |
| Lighting | soft/hard, direction, reflective surface control | impossible product finish, lost textures |
| Context | concrete occasion/use scene | irrelevant stock lifestyle image |
| Human presence | hands, wearer, creator, customer (with rights) | fabricated endorsement or impossible fit |
| Typography | exact words, hierarchy, alignment, contrast, duration | 5 lines of fine print on a phone |
| Color | real product fidelity + brand palette | hue manipulation to look like a different variant |
| Movement | reveal, turn, zoom, hand action, pace, loop | meaningless motion, shaky illegibility |
| Proof | zoom, spec, side-by-side, disclosure, authentic quote | fake or distorted evidence |

## Static image patterns

- **Product hero** for direct recognition: product large, recognizable, useful detail immediately clear.
- **In-use/lifestyle** for desired context: product stays legible; avoid product becoming a tiny background accessory.
- **Detail/quality** for consideration: macro feature + accurate materials/specification only when verified.
- **Offer/merchandising** for demand capture: SKU, actual price or term, appropriately readable; don't overclaim urgency.
- **Collection/variety** for exploration: organized comparisons, not a collage of dozens of postage-stamp images.
- **Comparison** for decision support: same camera scale and honest criteria, both sides represented fairly.

## Design rules of thumb

- **One primary message per asset.** One headline/overlay + one proof cue is usually easier to understand than multiple benefits in competing type sizes.
- **Readability:** test phone-size previews, low brightness, lower-resolution compression, screen readers/captions where supported. Favor short overlay words, contrast, enough hold time, and avoid text under app UI.
- **Contrast guidance:** use WCAG 4.5:1 normal text / 3:1 large text as a *practical legibility benchmark*, not a claim that every rendered ad is legally subject to web-content WCAG. [W3C-derived guidance](https://www.section508.gov/develop/guide-accessible-web-design-development/).
- **Brand signals:** repeat a distinctive but grounded identity across variants; ensure branding is not mistaken for a promise (e.g. prestige imagery masquerading as a verifiable quality claim).
- **Image truth:** no invented packaging, altered hardware, extra stones, impossible material, fake product detail or AI-composited human use implying an actual customer experience.

## Creative review: diagnose rather than say 'make it pop'

State *what* fails, *where*, *why*, and *the change*:

`At feed thumbnail size the ring occupies 9% of frame, so detailing is illegible; move to a 3/4 macro crop occupying roughly half the frame while retaining accurate scale context. Keep wordmark in upper safe center.`

This is a production hypothesis, not a measured performance fact. Validate with mobile previews and tests.
