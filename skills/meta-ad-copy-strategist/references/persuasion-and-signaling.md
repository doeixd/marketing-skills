# Persuasion and signaling: make value understandable and believable

**Use when:** deciding how an ad might change attention, belief, preference or action, particularly when buyers cannot directly assess quality before purchase.

## Choose a persuasion mechanism that fits the decision

### Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)

Petty and Cacioppo distinguish argument-focused, effortful evaluation from reliance on simpler contextual cues. They are **processes** influenced by motivation and ability, not permanent 'rational' or 'irrational' customer types.

- When the product requires careful comparison or feels high stakes, provide substantive specifications, direct proof, relevant trade-offs and transparent limitations.
- When the purchase is quick or aesthetic, recognizability, imagery and simple category cues may carry more of the message; avoid misleading impressions.
- Good advertising can serve both: an appealing visual immediately identifiable as the brand, coupled with an accurate product argument for interested buyers.

### Persuasion Knowledge Model (PKM)

Friestad and Wright describe how people learn to recognize marketers' persuasive intentions and tactics, and how that changes their responses. Apply a **skeptical-customer test**:

- Does this sound like a person explaining useful value or a manipulation script?
- Is urgency genuine? Is the 'expert' actually qualified? Are reviewers authentic?
- Would the customer feel misled when seeing the price, product, or delivery terms?
- Does the message preserve agency and present an honest next step?

### Cialdini's seven principles: ethical application only

| Principle | Legitimate use | Not acceptable |
|---|---|---|
| Social proof | Accurate, relevant reviews with provenance | Invented testimonials or false counts |
| Authority | Genuine expertise, documented credentials | Fake endorsements or invented certification |
| Scarcity | Verified inventory / real expiry | False countdowns, permanent 'last chance' |
| Reciprocity | Truly useful guidance or genuine gift | Hidden obligation or bait-and-switch |
| Commitment / consistency | Clear opt-in and follow-through | Coercive pressure or trick questions |
| Liking | Human voice and respectful relevance | Fake familiarity or identity assumptions |
| Unity | Authentic shared community, when appropriate | Pretending to belong to a group |

Do **not** force a principle into every ad; clear product information may be the best persuasion.

## Signaling quality, trust and identity

Signals are *observable cues* customers use to infer less-observable attributes. A cue is not proof by itself.

| Signal | Possible inference | What actually substantiates it? |
|---|---|---|
| Detailed close-up photography | Product is transparent / carefully made | Authentic photos of the shipped SKU |
| Clear materials and measurements | Seller knows the product and reduces uncertainty | Correct verified specs |
| Warranty/returns | Buyer risk may be reduced | Real policy wording and fulfilment |
| Established brand history | Experience or continuity | Confirmed dates/history, not assumed quality |
| Higher price | Premium positioning or exclusivity | Actual product/service differentiation (price alone proves nothing) |
| Reviews | Others were satisfied | Genuine, relevant, sufficiently contextual customer feedback |
| Packaging / design cues | Taste, craftsmanship or gift suitability | Actual packaging and buyer reception |

Distinguish **quality signaling** (what this says about the good), **trust signaling** (what this says about the seller) and **identity signaling** (what choosing it says about the buyer). Each is subject to interpretation: one buyer's prestige cue is another's inflated price.

## Behavior: motivation, ability and prompt

The Fogg Behavior Model proposes that behavior requires sufficient motivation, ability and a prompt at the same time. Use this as a diagnosis, not a pseudo-scientific score:

- **Motivation:** value, occasion, desire and relevance.
- **Ability:** affordability, availability, clarity, compatibility, ease of checkout, delivery.
- **Prompt:** CTA, useful reminder, relevant placement or occasion.

If ability is low (no sizing information, unavailable stock, impossible delivery), increasing urgency or emotional language may worsen frustration. Recommend fixing the barrier rather than maximizing 'pressure'.

## The nonlinear decision journey

Google's *Messy Middle* describes repeated exploration and evaluation between triggers and purchases. Creative may need to support discovering options, comparison, reassurance, recall or immediate purchase. Do not infer a person's stage from a single impression; use campaign context and test outcomes.

## Create mechanism-aware angles

For each proposed angle record:

`buying_situation | desired_progress | belief_or_uncertainty_to_change | persuasion_mechanism | message | evidence | creative_support | likely_backfire | test_metric`

Examples:

- **Comparison/clarity:** verified sizing info reduces decision cost; useful to product-aware shoppers.
- **Identity/aesthetic:** real design shown clearly enables self-expression; not proof every buyer seeks identity.
- **Reassurance:** real return terms could lower perceived risk; don't advertise 'risk-free' without qualification.
- **Recognition:** recognizable brand cue plus gift occasion builds retrievable memory; long-term effect cannot be inferred from one CTR report.
