# Creative quality assurance: a production and editorial rubric

**Two separate gates:** (A) hard stops for truth, authorization, rights and basic usability; (B) quality ratings for hypotheses to improve. A high creative score never overrides a policy violation or fabricated product claim.

## A. Hard stop: fix before release

- Product, offer, price, SKU, variants, stock and landing page actually match.
- Objective claims, percentages, comparisons, warranties, shipping times and endorsements substantiated in evidence ledger.
- Image/video accurately depicts product and materials. Generated changes don't create false features, size, real-life use or a fabricated person/customer.
- Music, talent, creator, image, asset licenses and required disclosure are cleared for paid placements.
- No discriminatory/sensitive-personal-attribute assumptions, misleading UI or counterfeit interaction/button overlays; check current Meta policy and local legal rules.
- All essential text/disclosures visible and readable in selected placements; review phone-size and muted version as appropriate.
- Brand owner explicitly approves final claim, creative versions, enhancements and spend before publishing.

## B. Creative scorecard (0 = absent, 1 = weak, 2 = adequate, 3 = strong)

| Criterion | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|
| Buyer relevance | Does it reflect a credible desire or buying occasion? |
| One-message clarity | Can viewer explain the central point in one sentence? |
| Visual comprehension | Product/action visible quickly at mobile scale? |
| Claim/proof alignment | Are benefits evidenced visually or by verified source? |
| Brand attribution | Brand recognizable through actual distinctive assets early enough? |
| Placement fitness | Correct format/crop, safe zones, first-frame/audio/captions? |
| Natural communication | Appropriate diction/idioms without hype, forced trend or overtext? |
| Purchase handoff | Next step and landing page fit offer and motivation? |

Total /24. **Do not treat score bands as predictive ROAS**. Use category-specific reviewer notes with `[issue → impact → revision → how to test]`.

## C. Four review modes

- **Silent/muted:** Hide captions/primary text if needed; does footage convey category and benefit? Then mute and see whether captions carry speech meaning.
- **First-frame/thumbnail:** At mobile feed thumbnail, is product identifiable? Is branding discernible?
- **Placement UI:** Preview Instagram Feed, Stories and Reels (and selected Facebook placements) with account header, CTA, right rail, caption and cropping; text can disappear despite valid file specs.
- **Combination/enhancements:** Preview every available primary text/headline pairing and auto-modified creative; reject misleading text moves/overlays/background changes.

## D. Failure diagnosis → correction

| Observation | Likely creative issue (hypothesis) | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| High video views, low qualified clicks | Entertaining but irrelevant hook / no clear offer | Product/benefit in first scene; explicit relevant handoff |
| Product looks smaller than expected | Weak scale cues or crop | Add genuine in-hand/measurement shot; correct PDP dimensions |
| Users can't identify advertiser | Brand linkage too late/generic | Add authentic product/packaging or named brand cue early |
| Good click rate, poor purchases | Offer/PDP/traffic mismatch as well as possible creative | Audit landing page, shipping, SKU and purchase intent |
| Comments question materials or fit | Proof not provided / product info weak | Film verified close-up or size demonstration, update PDP |
| Story CTA covers overlay | Unsafe text placement | Move critical words into visible center and re-preview |
| AI-enhanced image changes SKU | Alteration of product truth | Disable/reject enhancement; use original verified asset |
| Creator seems like paid actor | Staged tone or insufficient disclosure | Use honest real experience and clear partnership disclosure |

**Human review remains required.** Automated checks can catch missing metadata, dimensions or source fields, but cannot certify that a product is truthfully depicted or that a concept will sell.
