# Worked example — from an unfamiliar offering to a qualified marketing plan

**All details below are fictional placeholders unless explicitly marked as hypothetical.** The examples demonstrate the *method*, not claims about a real company. Compare against [Offering Discovery Workflow 1A](../workflows/01a-offering-discovery-and-marketing-plan.md).

## Example A: Ornate vintage-inspired ring

**Scope:** One physical product, not the entire jewelry brand. Owner has supplied one product photo and a listing naming the product "vintage-inspired patterned ring." No real customer reviews, competitor list, materials specification, price, size options, shipping/return policies or brand identity are provided.

### 1. What do we know?

| ID | Fact / interpretation | Status | Marketing permission |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Listing calls it vintage-inspired | Seller-stated; inspect official listing | Use with attribution if accurate |
| F2 | Photo shows patterned ornamental detailing | Observed (fictional scenario) | Describe what is visible |
| H1 | Some buyers might want expressive everyday jewelry | Hypothesis | Test as a creative angle, not a customer statistic |
| H2 | Some may choose it as a personal gift | Hypothesis | Test a gifting concept, not a "most popular gift" claim |
| U1 | Exact material, quality, weight, fit, finish and manufacture | Unknown | No claims |
| U2 | Price, delivery, policy, reviews, brand story | Unknown | No claims |

**Brand scan:** no evidence sufficient to discover the actual brand voice. Use neutral provisional copy, request official brand materials, and run [Brand Discovery 3A](../workflows/03a-brand-discovery-and-messaging.md) before scaling.

### 2. Job/choice hypotheses

| Situation | Candidate desire | What they might choose instead | Potential reason not to buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Style browsing | Something visibly expressive | Minimalist ring or no ring | Pattern too ornate |
| Gift consideration | Something suited to recipient taste | Other accessory or gift card | Unknown fit, time to arrive, recipient preference |
| Product comparison | Style and details relative to cost | Similar jewelry from another store | Unverified material, price or returns |

**Evidence needed:** buyer interviews/reviews, onsite queries, purchase occasions, product comparison pages. These are not demonstrated customer segments.

### 3. Feature → benefit → tradeoff

- **Patterned ornamental surface** (F2) → more visually detailed appearance (observable) → possible fit for someone preferring expressive styling (H1) → **tradeoff:** unsuitable for buyers wanting a simple/plain ring.
- **Vintage-inspired aesthetic** (F1) → evokes a particular visual tradition → potential identity/occasion message (hypothesis) → **tradeoff:** not a universal preference.
- **Unknown material** (U1) → no justified durability/hypoallergenic/heirloom claim → **action:** get exact materials and product photographs.

**Not permitted:** "handcrafted," "tarnish-proof," "nickel-free," "heirloom quality," "last chance," "free shipping," real customer sentiments or prices. None are in evidence.

### 4. Prioritized marketing action

1. **P0:** obtain accurate specifications, scale photography, actual size guidance, pricing, shipping and return policy; make this clear on PDP.
2. **P1:** compare real alternatives and validate which buying occasions create demand. Update brand guide or create provisional voice with owner approval.
3. **P2:** build three distinct creatives (product detail, styling/identity, gift selection), each clearly experimental and pointing to an accurate page. Evaluate product-level purchases, contribution, returns and questions—do not optimize for CTR alone.

### 5. Copy bank, *provisional and claim-limited*

**One-line definition:** "A vintage-inspired ring with ornamental patterned detail."

**Benefit-oriented bullets:**
- **Vintage-inspired design** — a recognizable ornamental look. (F1)
- **Patterned surface detail** — a visually expressive design. (F2)
- **Take a closer look** — see the pattern before choosing. (F2; request close-up images)

**Candidate headlines:**
- "Explore Vintage-Inspired Rings" *(only if a verified collection exists; otherwise singular "Ring")*
- "A Ring with Patterned Detail"
- "Look Closer at the Design"

**Candidate descriptions:** "See the details", "Explore the design".

**Meta concept 1 — product detail**
- Primary: "Patterned details and a vintage-inspired look. See the ring up close."
- Headline: "Look Closer at the Design"
- Description: "View ring details"
- Visual: authentic photo close-up, actual brand mark; no false material signal.

**Meta concept 2 — stylistic fit**
- Primary: "For anyone drawn to a little more detail. Explore this vintage-inspired ring."
- Headline: "A Ring with Character"
- Description: "See the design"
- Visual: verified-on-body styling if available; no fabricated wear/use photo.

**Meta concept 3 — gift consideration**
- Primary: "Choosing a ring for someone? Start with the details that match their style."
- Headline: "Explore the Ring"
- Description: "View design details"
- Visual: real product presentation; show gift packaging only if genuine.

**Important:** these concepts should not be published before brand verification, owner approval, accurate sale terms and destination checks. Their buyer motivations remain hypotheses.

## Example B: A two-week AI process review service

**Fictional service only** — demonstrates that service marketing needs different evidence.

### 1. Offering truth to verify
- Scope: "two-week review" could mean discovery meetings, workflow mapping, recommended changes and a delivered written report. Confirm exact inclusions, exclusions, number of sessions, schedule and effort.
- Proof: named team and experience (verify credentials), sample anonymized deliverable with permission, actual process steps, case studies with realistic context.
- Delivery: client data access, confidentiality, security, contract, budget, timeline, dependencies and implementation responsibilities.
- Price: fixed fee vs day-rate, number of hours, follow-on fees and revision limits.

### 2. Buyer and alternatives
- Operations manager might want process clarity, not "AI strategy." Buyer may be owner/finance lead.
- Alternatives: doing nothing, internal spreadsheet/workshop, specialist consultant, implementation agency.
- Potential objections: "Will this disrupt work?", "Will it expose confidential data?", "Is it just a generic ChatGPT report?", "Who implements recommendations?"
- Honest intervention: documented methodology, narrowly defined deliverables, sample report, consent/security policy and explicit "implementation not included" *if true*.

### 3. Message to test, conditional on confirmed scope

**Value proposition hypothesis:** "Identify which recurring workflows may be practical candidates for automation before committing to a larger build." Not a promise of savings.

**Possible page bullets (only after scope confirmed):**
- **Workflow inventory** — document current processes and identify repeatable tasks.
- **Opportunity assessment** — evaluate candidate automations, assumptions and constraints.
- **Prioritized recommendations** — deliver a practical next-step plan.

**Candidate headline:** "Know What to Automate First"

**Candidate primary text:** "Not every business task needs an AI agent. Start with an assessment of your workflows, constraints, and the opportunities worth pursuing."

**Critical missing facts:** prices, process, experience, deliverables, eligibility, evidence of customer needs and legal/privacy requirements. Publish no guarantees of ROI or time saved without substantiation.
