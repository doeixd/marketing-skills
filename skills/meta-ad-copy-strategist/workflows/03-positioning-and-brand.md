# Workflow 3 — Competitive positioning and brand memory

**Trigger:** Buyer evidence exists; the product seems interchangeable; price comparison is difficult; customers don't recognize the brand; new product/category entry.

**Specific offering?** For deep product/service understanding, use [Offering Discovery 1A](01a-offering-discovery-and-marketing-plan.md) and its [dossier](../templates/offering-dossier-and-marketing-plan.md) to supply a verified feature→benefit→tradeoff map, real competing alternatives, buyer situations and price/assurance context. Revisit this workflow to challenge the candidate positioning rather than accepting a nice-sounding tagline.

**Brand not yet understood?** Run [Workflow 3A — discover brand identity, voice, positioning and copy system](03a-brand-discovery-and-messaging.md) first. It separates owner aspiration, observed product/communication reality and buyer perception, then delivers a reusable copy bank. Use the [voice reference](../references/brand-voice-and-copy-system.md) and [messaging library](../templates/brand-messaging-library.md). Proceed with this competitive-positioning workflow to scrutinize the candidate promises against alternatives.

## A. Competitive positioning procedure

Follow **alternatives → capabilities → value → best-fit buying situations → useful category context**.

1. **Alternatives:** name realistic competitors, substitutes, doing nothing, and default habits. Include why they may win.
2. **Category parity:** identify basic things needed to be credible (e.g., real materials/sizing, fulfillment, price clarity). Distinguish genuine category expectations from invented benchmarks.
3. **Difference:** list attributes verified by data or observable product comparison; verify claims of superiority if used publicly.
4. **Benefit:** ask *so what for this buyer in this situation?* Make a benefit ladder: `feature → consequence → valued outcome → evidence`.
5. **Fit and tradeoff:** state who especially values the difference, who may reject it, and where the competition is preferable.
6. **Category framing:** choose familiar language that helps buyers understand what is sold and what to compare it to.

Draft: **For [buying situation], [product/brand] is [category] that offers [relevant benefit] because [verified proof], compared with [credible alternative].** This is an internal strategy statement, not mandatory ad text.

## B. Brand memory plan

Distinguish three goals:

- **Distinctiveness:** recognizable name, shape, imagery, visual system, packaging, style and verbal cues.
- **Differentiation:** a reason to prefer the offering or a segment-specific advantage.
- **Meaning and trust:** what customers learn to expect and whether the brand reliably delivers it.

Map 3–5 relevant **category entry points** (gift occasion, home refresh, replacing a broken item, desire for a certain style, etc.). Document whether the brand is currently associated with each. A brand ad should connect a recognizable brand to an occasion or desired outcome; a conversion ad should still preserve cues when feasible. Avoid claiming that distinctiveness alone guarantees preference.

## C. Proof and objection matrix

| Proposed promise | Buyer significance | Proof needed | Tradeoff | Where to show it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design character | Self-expression | Accurate close-up images | May not suit minimalists | Ad and PDP |
| Durable construction | Longevity | Verified materials and supportable testing | Price/weight tradeoffs | PDP and claims sheet |
| Gift convenience | Reduced buying effort | Actual packaging, policy, delivery dates | Fulfillment limits | Gift creative and page |

The table is illustrative: no warranty, packaging, durability or delivery promises are presumed.

## D. Decision gate

**Proceed** when the category is clear, compelling reasons are relevant and verifiable, brand assets are selected, and copy can survive a skeptical comparison. **Reconsider** when differentiation is unsubstantiated, category parity is missing, or the claimed signal (premium, craftsmanship, sustainability) lacks evidence.
