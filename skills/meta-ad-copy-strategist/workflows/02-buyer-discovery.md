# Workflow 2 — Buyer, market and buying-situation discovery

**Trigger:** Unclear customer needs, inconsistent product response, new category, high-curiosity/low-conversion creative, weak product claims.

## A. Gather observable data before imagining personas

1. First-party: product reviews (including negatives), questions and support conversations, onsite searches, sales/returns, survey/interview statements, repeated objections. Observe permission/privacy constraints.
2. Market: genuine competing offers, customer comparison language, search queries, category expectations, price/availability; competitor ads show messaging, **not measured effectiveness**.
3. Behavior: product views, add-to-cart, cart abandonment, repeat purchase and gift-season patterns; don't assume causal explanations from analytics alone.
4. Explicit unknowns: record where there are no interviews/reviews and where a motive is merely plausible.

## B. The buyer-decision map

For each distinct **buying occasion**, answer:

| Question | Output for marketing |
|---|---|
| When does the category become relevant? | Category entry point and campaign theme |
| Who buys vs uses vs receives it? | Buyer role and appropriate visuals/copy |
| What progress or pleasure is sought? | Functional/emotional/social/identity value hypotheses |
| What is currently unsatisfying? | Push force—but don't invent a pain point for a pleasure purchase |
| What attracts them to this offering? | Pull force and feature-benefit candidate |
| What might they regret? | Anxiety/objection and risk reduction |
| What prevents change? | Inertia, alternative habits or nonpurchase |
| What alternatives would they choose? | Real competitive set and possible positioning |
| What details determine the decision? | Must-have product info and comparison criteria |
| What proof would settle uncertainty? | Review, measurement, demonstration, specification or policy requirement |

Phrase JTBD as `When [situation], I want [progress], so I can [desired result]` and separately tag each element's evidence. Avoid demographic stereotypes, highly sensitive inferences, and claims that users personally have vulnerabilities.

## C. Collect exact language, not manufactured quotes

Build a phrase bank of actual customer wording with source identifiers and consent/usage considerations. Classify phrases as desire, benefit, objection, alternative, evaluation criterion or outcome. Do **not** fabricate testimonials or present AI-generated interpretations as spoken statements. Prefer frequency/cross-source convergence over memorable one-offs.

## D. Synthesize hypotheses

Construct 2–4 candidate buying situations and rank each using:

`relevance to actual customers × strength of product fit × reachability × available evidence × commercial feasibility`

This is a heuristic for prioritization, **not a calibrated probability**. Document counterevidence and cases where a feature is a drawback for some customers. Distinguish 'best-fit situations' from excluding broad category buyers; a brand-building campaign may intentionally reach beyond the immediate niche.

## E. Decision gate and handoff

- **Enough to write ads:** at least one credible situation, a desired outcome, plausible alternative, grounded benefit and approved claims.
- **Not enough to assert a trend:** a single review or unsupported persona is not a market-wide truth; run interviews, surveys or bounded creative tests.
- **If recurring complaints indicate a product flaw:** route to [offer/product improvements](04-offer-and-conversion.md), not spin.

**Handoff:** buyer occasion map + evidence ledger + objection register → [positioning](03-positioning-and-brand.md) and [creative](06-creative-production.md). See [buyer research](../references/buyer-research.md).
