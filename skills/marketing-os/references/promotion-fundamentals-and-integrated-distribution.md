This guide builds on [Brand Discovery 3A](../workflows/03a-brand-discovery-and-messaging.md), [Offering Discovery 1A](../workflows/01a-offering-discovery-and-marketing-plan.md), [Buyer Discovery 2](../workflows/02-buyer-discovery.md), [Channel Plan 5](../workflows/05-channel-and-campaign.md), [Creative 6](../workflows/06-creative-production.md), and [Measurement 8](../workflows/08-measure-diagnose-experiment.md).

## 1. Promotion vs the rest of marketing

**Promotion** conveys an offering/brand's relevance, builds availability and trust, helps buyer decisions, and creates opportunities for action. It cannot create a viable product, accurate price, safe claims or a working checkout. Distinguish **strategic marketing** (buyers, positioning, economics, distribution) from **promotional execution** (messages, partnerships, media, content and amplification). Focus on *whose behavior or understanding needs to change* before proposing a channel.

Pick a purpose: category/brand memory among future buyers; awareness of a real event/new offer; understanding of a feature; confidence in quality; comparison; demand capture; activation; postpurchase support; retention or advocacy. Match each to an actual customer and metric. A launch is not by itself a news story.

## 2. PESO: four overlapping distribution modes

Adapted conceptually from **Gini Dietrich / Spin Sucks PESO** (paid, earned, shared, owned); it is a planning lens, not a universal allocation formula.

| Mode | Examples | Control / cost | Strong uses | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paid** | Meta/TikTok/search ads; creator sponsorships, paid boosting; sponsored newsletters/podcasts | Purchases reach or placement; creative remains subject to rules | Scale a clear message or reach a definable audience | Paid coverage is **not independent editorial endorsement**; attribution can overstate incrementality |
| **Earned** | Independent journalists, legitimate reviews, unsolicited mentions, organic creator coverage, expert citations | Third parties control whether/what they say | Credibility, relevance, niche awareness | Coverage and links cannot be promised or purchased as "earned" |
| **Shared** | Social posts, discussions, collaborations, community participation, referrals | Platform controls distribution; community controls response | Build relationships and facilitate spread | Follows/shares ≠ purchase intent; platform access can change |
| **Owned** | Website/PDP, email to authorized subscribers, blog, newsletter, company video and media room | Brand controls source-of-truth and presentation | Accurate explanation, conversion, long-term resource | Audience must still be acquired; rented social profiles aren't the same as owned property |

Integrate modes: **original owned asset → platform-native shared excerpts → credible partner/editorial outreach → selective paid amplification → clear owned destination → feedback and improvement**. This is not a mandatory linear funnel. Check actual strengths, audience, budget, permissions and evidence before recommending every mode.

## 3. Communication and persuasion mechanics

Every promotional unit answers:
1. **Who is it for and what situation makes it relevant?**
2. **What do they notice first?** (visual, verbal, headline, headline/thumbnail match)
3. **What one thing should they understand, feel, remember, question, or do?**
4. **Why should they believe it?** (demonstration, documentation, real lived experience, source)
5. **Why share it?** Useful information, identity fit, emotion, surprise, social currency or practical value.
6. **Where does the next action happen?** (website, store, event, reply, signup, consultation)
7. **What are the tradeoffs or reasonable objections?**
8. **How do we tell whether the communication accomplished its purpose?**

Avoid pretending a "hook" is a proven sale. Effective attention is **relevant and attributable**, not merely large. Show the product/brand early enough that attention benefits the actual advertiser. Distinguish future buyers (brand-building) from in-market buyers (activation) per **Binet and Field**; their aggregate results are not mandatory platform ratios.

## 4. Story/news/creative idea territories

Choose territories based on verifiable real events or buyer-relevant truths:
- Product demonstration, design/process detail, explainer, useful instruction, honest comparison.
- Founder/employee perspective and behind-the-scenes **only when genuine**.
- Customer experience, before/after with representative substantiation, or real use case with permission.
- Occasion and identity; seasonality that fits real buyer needs rather than made-up urgency.
- Original research/data, public event, collaboration, verified milestone, community activity, earned recognition.
- Mistake/lesson learned, limitations or common buyer misconception, when accurate and not exploitative.

For each choose **message pillar, channel-native format, proof asset, brand cue, buyer and target outcome**. Test several *different premises* instead of ten synonyms. Do not claim "viral," "guaranteed coverage," "guaranteed reach," or infer what a competitor's content earned commercially.

## 5. Planning fundamentals

Create an integrated brief with:
- **Objective:** behavioral/brand outcome and time horizon.
- **Audience:** buying occasions, media usage, trust sources, likely objections and accessibility requirements.
- **Reality:** verified brand/offering claims, price/terms, capacity, asset rights, competitor positioning.
- **Big idea:** one clear organizing premise; 2–4 distinct creative/news/partner angles.
- **Channel roles:** platform/channel is chosen because *this buyer uses it for this job*; define owned landing destination and message match.
- **Assets:** hero content, derivatives, photographs/demo, source data, creator partners, FAQs, press kit.
- **Resources and constraints:** labor, creator/wire fees, paid media budget, approvals, legal, customer support, timeline, translation and brand safety.
- **Sequencing:** prepare proof/destination → announce or seed → publish/engage → optional amplify → measure/iterate; adapt by campaign.
- **Gates:** truthful claims, real buyer fit, accessible assets, contracts/consent, brand safety, checkout/capacity, tracking and authorization.
- **Metrics:** see AMEC below; distinguish activities from impacts.

## 6. Guru/framework triage — useful lenses, not commandments

| Name/framework | What can be useful | What NOT to infer |
|---|---|---|
| **Gini Dietrich / PESO** | Coordinate paid, earned, shared and owned work around one message | Four channels are equally valuable or every small team needs all four |
| **Les Binet & Peter Field** | Balance long-term brand-building with short-term activation and different metrics | A fixed budget split optimizes every brand and platform |
| **Jonah Berger / STEPPS** | Social currency, triggers, emotion, public visibility, practical value and stories as sharing prompts | A checklist guarantees virality |
| **Gary Vaynerchuk** | Document genuine work and repurpose substantial pillar material natively | Posting six times daily is mandatory, free or proven optimal |
| **Cialdini/ELM** | Match persuasion and proof to audience involvement; real authority/social proof | Manipulative scarcity/fake experts are acceptable |
| **Mark Ritson / STP** | Research and segmentation → choose target → relative positioning before channel tactics | Demographics alone determine customer choice |
| **Hormozi-like offer and hook heuristics** | Clarify desired outcome, perceived likelihood, time/effort and reason to pay attention | Hyperbole, fabricated "results", rigid hook formulas are validated science |

Use primary or credible research before claiming a tactic works. Influencer advice varies widely by business and platform; always contrast with customer truth, channel behavior and commercial results.

## 7. Promotion measurements: AMEC logic

AMEC's **Integrated Evaluation Framework** and Barcelona Principles distinguish:
- **Inputs/activities:** spend, effort, outreach, posts, media kits.
- **Outputs:** placements, impressions, posts, reach, attendance, published coverage.
- **Out-takes:** attention, recall, understanding, consideration/engagement.
- **Outcomes:** trust, preference, intent, meaningful action, customer behavior.
- **Impact:** attributable business/reputation/community effects where evidence permits.

For each promotional action set **primary KPI, secondary signals, source, baseline, time horizon, confounders and decision rule**. Earned coverage volume, AVE ("advertising value equivalency"), follower count, views and inflated "potential reach" are not evidence of incrementality or profit. Track net sales contribution, qualified leads, brand recall, share of search or other purpose-fit outcomes when feasible.
