# Product value, benefits, price and purchase friction

**Use when:** translating specifications into value, handling price objections, making offers, diagnosing poor conversion, or deciding whether the product itself needs improvement.

## Don't equate features with buyer value

For each product feature build a **means–end ladder**:

`Observed attribute → actual consequence → functional/aesthetic/social/emotional benefit → desired progress → evidence and caveat`

Example (illustrative, not product research):

- **Attribute:** the actual photograph depicts an ornate ring (verify SKU/product).
- **Consequence:** it looks different from a plain band.
- **Potential benefit:** gives someone an expressive style choice.
- **Potential desired progress:** find an accessory that feels personal.
- **Evidence gap:** whether customers actually buy for self-expression remains a hypothesis.

A ladder is not a license to make unverified emotional promises. One feature may support multiple benefits for separate buyers and may be a *drawback* for another buyer.

## Functional, experiential, social and symbolic value

- **Functional:** usefulness, fit, reliability, durability, compatibility, performance, convenience.
- **Aesthetic/experiential:** appearance, sensory enjoyment, discovery, delight, collecting.
- **Emotional:** confidence, enjoyment, reassurance, meaning, sentiment; characterize as buyer hypotheses until supported.
- **Social/identity:** belonging, taste, status or thoughtful gifting; not necessarily universal or explicitly conscious.
- **Brand/service:** recognizable seller, helpful support, transparent policies, selection, delivery experience.

For aspirational and artistic products, pleasure and identity can be real motivations even without a tangible 'problem'. Avoid fake pain points.

## Perceived value includes sacrifice and risk

Zeithaml's price/quality/value model is an important reminder: customers compare **what they believe they get** with **what they believe they give up**. Consider money, shipping, time, effort, uncertainty, return hassle, maintenance, and opportunity cost, rather than treating discounts as the only price lever.

| Purchase uncertainty | Possible buyer response | Better intervention |
|---|---|---|
| 'Will it fit?' | Avoid or delay | Accurate measurements, sizing help, verifiable exchange terms |
| 'Will it look like the image?' | Hesitate | Honest multi-angle imagery/video |
| 'Why this price?' | Compare alternatives | Clarify real materials, design, service, total value |
| 'Will it arrive in time?' | Buy elsewhere | Actual estimates and fulfillment improvement |
| 'Do I trust this shop?' | Seek reviews or choose familiar retailer | Authentic proof, clear policies, transparent business details |
| 'Will the recipient like it?' | Choose safer gift | Real packaging/return/gift details; clear style variety |

Classify each objection as **information gap**, **misperception**, **real product weakness**, **preference mismatch**, **offer mismatch**, or **external constraint**. Copy is sometimes appropriate; sometimes it would be deceptive to paper over the issue.

## Pricing is not proof of quality

Some customers may infer exclusivity or quality from higher prices. The signaling literature models conditions where price or advertising spend can carry information about unobservable quality; it does **not** show that expensive goods are automatically superior. Avoid unsupported 'luxury' or 'premium' inference unless brand/product positioning and evidence fit.

## Offer construction and ethical urgency

A believable offer specifies actual product, total price, qualifying conditions, fulfillment, deadlines if any, and what the buyer receives. Genuine limited stock or time restrictions can be stated **only when true**, not engineered as an invented psychological trigger. Do not obscure exclusions or push customers into unwanted purchases.

## Decision: what to do about a drawback

- **Explain** if accurate information exists and the concern is informational.
- **Demonstrate** if a product photograph, measurement or test resolves uncertainty.
- **Acknowledge/segment** if it is a preference trade-off: ornate vs minimalist, heavy vs lightweight.
- **Change the offering** if durability, reliability, size options, cost or delivery genuinely disappoint customers.
- **Defer claim or ad** if proof, safety, authorization or legal compliance is unresolved.
