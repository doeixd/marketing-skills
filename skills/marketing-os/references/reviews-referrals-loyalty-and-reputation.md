# Reviews, referrals, loyalty and reputation (reviewed 2026-11-09)

Used by [Workflow 9F](../workflows/09f-advocacy-reviews-referrals-and-reputation.md), [5N local](../workflows/05n-local-marketing-and-reviews.md) and [5M marketplaces](../workflows/05m-marketplace-listings-and-ads.md). This is operating guidance, not legal advice; rules differ by country and platform.

## 1. What the law and platforms prohibit

**US FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** (16 CFR Part 465, in effect since October 2024) allows civil penalties per violation (the FTC's December 2025 warning letters cite up to $53,088). It prohibits:

- fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones and ones from people who never used the product;
- buying or rewarding reviews **conditioned on a particular sentiment** (positive or negative);
- reviews by officers, managers, employees or their relatives without clear disclosure, and soliciting such reviews;
- company-controlled sites presented as independent reviews;
- **review suppression**: threats or intimidation to prevent or remove negative reviews, or implying displayed reviews are all reviews when negative ones were withheld;
- buying or selling fake followers, views or other social-influence indicators.

The FTC [Endorsement Guides](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255) additionally require disclosure of any incentive or material connection. The UK (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024) and the EU (Unfair Commercial Practices Directive as amended) ban fake reviews and require traders to say how they check that reviews are genuine.

**Platform policies are stricter than the law:**

| Platform | Key rules |
|---|---|
| Google (Business Profile / Maps) | No incentives of any kind; no review gating; reported April 2026 additions: no on-premises pressure or kiosks, no staff quotas, no telling customers what to write |
| Amazon | No incentives, no asking for positive reviews, no selective solicitation; use "Request a Review" and Vine |
| Yelp | Don't ask for reviews at all |
| Trustpilot and similar | Invite all customers the same way; incentives must be disclosed and unconditional |

## 2. Asking for reviews well

- Trigger after real use: delivery + enough days to try it; after the job is finished; after an activation milestone.
- Same request to everyone; plain subject; direct link; under a minute to complete.
- Ask specific prompts ("What were you looking for? How does it fit or perform? Anything you'd tell someone deciding?"), which produce reviews that help buyers, without scripting the answer.
- One reminder at most. Honor channel consent (a review request can be a marketing message under some laws).

## 3. Responding to reviews and comments

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Positive | Thank specifically; no keyword stuffing; don't offer a reward |
| Negative and legitimate | Reply promptly; acknowledge the specific issue; say what you've done or will do; move details to a private channel; follow up; never reveal personal or order details publicly |
| Negative and mistaken | Correct the fact politely with evidence a reader can check; no sarcasm |
| Suspected fake, spam, abusive or wrong business | Flag through the platform's process; reply briefly and neutrally if it stays up; don't accuse a real customer |
| Question in ad or social comments | Answer it; unanswered questions and unaddressed "scam" claims depress ad performance |

Hide or delete only spam, abuse, personal data or policy violations, under a written policy. Readers judge the reply as much as the review: a calm, specific response to a bad review builds trust.

## 4. Referral program design checklist

- Reward both sides; reward type matched to the product (credit for repeat categories, cash or gift for one-off purchases).
- Reward earned after the referred order clears returns; caps per referrer; exclusions for self-referral and existing customers.
- Unit economics: `(referrer reward + friend discount + platform cost) ÷ incremental referred customers ≤ allowable CAC`.
- Unique links or codes; decide how referral credit interacts with ads and affiliates so one sale isn't paid for twice.
- Terms: eligibility, fraud, changes, expiry. Tell referrers to disclose the reward when recommending publicly.
- Promote in the post-delivery email, account area, packaging and after a positive review.

## 5. Loyalty program cautions

- Many programs mostly reward purchases that would have happened. Test with a holdout group and report **incremental** repeat rate and margin.
- Points are a financial liability; model cost at realistic redemption.
- Simpler is better: a clear benefit a customer can explain in one sentence.
- Non-discount benefits (early access, free alterations, priority service) protect margin and price perception.
- State expiry and change terms clearly and give notice before devaluing.

## 6. Severity matrix for issues

| Level | Signs | Owner and timing | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine | Single complaint, question or low rating | Front-line, within normal response window | Reply, resolve, log theme |
| Elevated | Repeated theme, influential poster, customer harm or loss, mistaken ad or price | Marketing lead + operations, same day | Fix cause, personal outreach, public reply, pause related ads |
| Crisis | Safety, legal, privacy or data incident; discrimination or misconduct allegation; viral or press attention | Owner/executive + legal, within hours | Crisis protocol in [9F](../workflows/09f-advocacy-reviews-referrals-and-reputation.md#6-monitor-and-respond) |

**Holding statement pattern:** what we know → what we are doing now → what affected people should do → when we will update next. No speculation, no blame, no minimizing.
