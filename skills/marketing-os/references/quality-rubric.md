# Copy and strategy quality rubric

Use after drafting. This is an **internal editorial diagnostic**, not a validated estimate of future sales. Do not present the score as scientific or predict Meta approval.

## Score each dimension 0–2

| Dimension | 0 — fails | 1 — partial | 2 — strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer relevance | No identifiable purchase context | Plausible but vague | Specific relevant motive or occasion |
| Clear product | Reader cannot tell what it is | Category implied | Product/offer understood immediately |
| Tangible benefit | Adjective soup / features only | Some result indicated | Concrete, meaningful reason to care |
| Differentiation | Any competitor could say it | Some distinction | True, salient, defensible distinction |
| Credibility | Unsupported factual promises | Some claims uncertain | Key claims have traceable proof |
| Objection handling | Ignores central blocker | Mentions it without answer | Addresses or properly delegates it to verified proof |
| Field coherence | Repetition/contradiction | Mostly connected | Primary, headline, optional description reinforce one point |
| Tone | Clichés, hype, robotic | Acceptable | Natural, brand-appropriate, precise |
| Destination fit | Promise cannot be met | Partial fit | Claim can be verified at destination |
| Test value | Synonym of another ad | Some angle difference | Distinct mechanism with falsifiable hypothesis |

Score maximum 20, but **hard gates override scores**: unsupported material/safety/price claim, misleading offer, sensitive-personal-attribute risk, missing destination match, or real product misrepresentation means revise/block first.

## Suggested review loop

1. Rate privately; describe the **most consequential** weaknesses.
2. Check what buyer question remains unanswered.
3. Revisit product truth and evidence before editing wording.
4. Rewrite one concept's core message if needed, not merely add synonyms.
5. Re-run claim/policy and combination checks.

## Style cleanup

Avoid generic abstractions without supporting detail: "elevate", "unlock", "the ultimate", "perfect for everyone", "game-changer", "discover the difference", "premium quality". They are not automatically prohibited; they are usually low-information. Prefer a demonstrable attribute, concrete usage, meaningful occasion or authentic customer phrase.

Read aloud. Remove unnecessary filler. Use sentence structure appropriate to brand rather than a universal template. Keep the first sentence self-contained. Do not force pain amplification on a pleasure-driven category.

## Skeptical buyer test

Answer: (a) What is being sold? (b) Why might someone in the target situation want it? (c) What would make them hesitate? (d) What evidence supports the message? (e) Why here rather than elsewhere or not at all? (f) What will happen after clicking?

If the ad cannot answer (a), (b), and (f) clearly — or the product page cannot answer (c) and (d) — revise the campaign system, not just the punctuation.
