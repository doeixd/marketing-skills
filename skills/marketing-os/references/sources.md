# Annotated research library

This is an annotated source index for the **marketing-strategy and Meta ad copy skill**. The references are *interpretive lenses*, not instructions to imitate authority, manufacture customer motives, or claim guaranteed results.

Last reviewed: **2026-11-06**. This is a *research reading list*, not a guarantee every page remains public or that specific Meta product behavior is permanent. Prioritize current official Meta specifications and live account previews.

## Primary platform documentation and education

1. **[Meta Advertising Standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/)** — authoritative starting point for prohibited or restricted ad content, misleading claims, sensitive personal attributes, advertiser responsibilities. Review policy directly before publishing; public pages may have access restrictions.
2. **[Meta Ads Guide](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide)** — current creative specs by objective, format and placement. Some pages require login. Do not infer a universal strict character cap from a third-party article.
3. **[Meta Blueprint — Tips for Writing Effective Ad Copy](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/293804-tips-for-writing-effective-ad-copy)** (2022) — official short lesson on clear, concise messages that work with visuals.
4. **[Meta Blueprint — Ad creative that converts: how to diversify your ads for better results](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/707402)** (May 18, 2026) — official training on changing **formats, visuals and messages**; developing volume from current assets and AI-enabled creative.
5. **[Meta Blueprint — Designing A/B tests, creative tests and lift studies](https://formedia.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/219763-ab-test-conversion-brand-lift-course)** — test design, interpretation, confidence scores and inconclusive outcomes.
6. **[Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/)** — explore live competitor ads and message patterns. For ordinary commercial ads it does **not** provide a reliable ROAS or purchase-conversion leaderboard.

## Marketing strategy and foundations

17. **[April Dunford — A Quickstart Guide to Positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)** — primary explanation of alternatives-first positioning: competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → best-fit customers → category. Emphasizes the actual alternatives prospects consider, not theoretical competitors. See [market-positioning.md](market-positioning.md).
18. **[Christensen Institute — Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/resources/theory/jobs-to-be-done/)** — the progress consumers seek in circumstances; useful alongside rather than instead of observable purchase data. See [buyer-research.md](buyer-research.md).
19. **[Keller (1993) — Conceptualizing, Measuring, and Managing Customer-Based Brand Equity](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224299305700101)** — brand awareness/associations and their effect on responses to marketing. Original academic source. See [brand-strategy.md](brand-strategy.md).
20. **[Ehrenberg-Bass — Identifying and Prioritising Category Entry Points](https://marketingscience.info/learn-with-us/commercial-research/identifying-and-prioritising-category-entry-points)** — category purchase cues and mental availability, with empirical measurement focus. See [brand-strategy.md](brand-strategy.md).
21. **[Ehrenberg-Bass — Category Entry Points in B2B](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world)** — distinguishes mental and physical availability, cue-linked retrieval and brand memory. B2B context; avoid assuming numeric generalization to jewelry ecommerce.
22. **[Ehrenberg-Bass — Commercial Research](https://marketingscience.info/learn-with-us/commercial-research)** — distinctive brand assets, CEPs and category growth analysis. Distinctiveness is not synonymous with differentiation.
23. **[Zeithaml (1988) — Consumer Perceptions of Price, Quality, and Value](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224298805200302)** — perceived value and sacrifice, perceived quality and pricing. See [product-value-and-pricing.md](product-value-and-pricing.md).
24. **[Milgrom & Roberts (1986) — Price and Advertising Signals of Product Quality](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/261408)** — theoretical conditions where price and advertising expenditure act as quality signals; not proof higher prices or spend mean better products. See [persuasion-and-signaling.md](persuasion-and-signaling.md).
25. **[Petty & Cacioppo (1986) — The Elaboration Likelihood Model of Persuasion](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(08)60214-2)** — argument scrutiny versus peripheral inference depending on elaboration; not permanent consumer types.
26. **[Friestad & Wright (1994) — Persuasion Knowledge Model](https://doi.org/10.1086/209380)** — consumers' recognition and interpretation of persuasive intent and tactics. Useful antidote to artificial urgency and aggressive gimmicks.
27. **[Robert Cialdini — Seven Principles of Persuasion](https://cialdini.com/principles)** — social proof, authority, liking, unity, scarcity, reciprocity, commitment/consistency; ethically use only real evidence and genuine terms.
28. **[Stanford — Fogg Behavior Model](https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model)** — motivation + ability + prompt; useful for recognizing conversion barriers ad copy alone can't fix.
29. **[Google — The Messy Middle](https://business.google.com/en-all/think/consumer-insights/navigating-purchase-behavior-and-decision-making/)** — non-linear exploration and evaluation behavior; Google-authored research, not a universal deterministic journey.
30. **[Baymard — Product Page UX Research](https://baymard.com/research/product-page)** — observed ecommerce usability issues and information gaps; relevant to choosing UX fixes over copy fixes.
31. **[IPA / Binet & Field — The Long and the Short of It](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation)** — long-term brand effects and short-term sales activation; don't turn averaged allocation findings into universal mandates.
32. **[IPA — Both or Else (2025)](https://ipa.co.uk/initiatives/effworks/effworks-ft-reports/both-or-else)** — updated commentary on combining brand building and activation rather than framing them as exclusive choices.

## Synthesis used by this skill (original guidance, not claimed as platform policy)

- **Research before copy**: specific buyer situation, desired outcomes, actual product details and source quality.
- **Benefit ladder**: attribute → real effect → buyer-valued benefit → desired progress; don't presume buyers all feel the same way.
- **Four forces**: investigate both reasons to move and reasons to delay/avoid purchasing.
- **Objection matrix**: distinguish a real drawback from a mere worry and choose evidence, better UX, acknowledgement or escalation.
- **Multiple angles**: favor **different buyer motivations** over superficial synonymous hooks.
- **Complementary fields**: primary text, headline and description each carry different value, with description optional.
- **Real experimentation**: connect each angle to a falsifiable hypothesis and purchase/qualified-lead metrics; avoid causal conclusions from unstructured ad results.
- **Ethical constraints**: no invented testimonials, urgency, quality, offers, sensitive-attribute assertions, or tricks that exploit vulnerabilities.

## What is *not* established by these sources

- A guaranteed optimal character length across all Meta placements.
- A universal highest-ROI hook or writing formula.
- That the Meta Ad Library reveals general commercial advertisers' winning campaigns.
- That high CTR necessarily means higher profitability.
- That AI-inferred motivations are facts about actual customers.
- That an AI's compliance checklist guarantees platform approval.
