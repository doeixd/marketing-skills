# PR, media relations, press releases, newswires and earned attention

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-06. Use [PR Workflow 5F](../workflows/05f-pr-earned-media-and-press-releases.md), [press release/pitch template](../templates/pr-newsroom-and-pitch.md), [promotion strategy](promotion-fundamentals-and-integrated-distribution.md) and [Brand Discovery](../workflows/03a-brand-discovery-and-messaging.md).

## 1. PR is a relationship and credibility discipline

Public relations creates mutual understanding, accurate public information and trust among audiences, media, partners, employees and communities. **Earned media** is independently selected editorial attention. A **press release** is a company-authored factual announcement; a **newswire** is a paid distribution service. A wire receipt or syndicated copy is **not** a guarantee of an independently reported article, editorial endorsement, SEO value, featured interview or positive coverage.

A useful newsroom may publish real releases, fact sheets, press contacts, authorized product photos, executive bios, safety/background info and actual interview availability. Do not present a company-written release as third-party journalism or buy covertly "earned" mentions.

## 2. Does the event actually meet a newsworthiness test?

Run a hard gate before generating a release:
- **Timeliness:** verifiable launch, public milestone, timely research or event.
- **Impact:** who is concretely affected, magnitude and relevance; substantiate all numbers.
- **Proximity:** local/community importance or jurisdiction-specific consequence.
- **Novelty:** genuinely new fact, useful original analysis, innovation with proof.
- **Prominence:** recognized organization/person *where verifiable*.
- **Human interest:** meaningful credible personal/community story with permissions.
- **Conflict/accountability:** an important issue responsibly framed, not manufactured controversy.

If there is no meaningful external news, propose **a buyer-helpful blog post, product announcement email, expert pitch on an active topic, behind-the-scenes social series or original useful study** instead of a fake press release.

## 3. Journalism vs marketing voice

Write from what the publication's readers need, not an SEO keyword count:
- Short factual headline identifying new event and why it matters; accurate subhead.
- Dateline and release date; who/what/where/when/why/how near top (**inverted pyramid**).
- News detail supported by documents, exact terms, relevant data/method and limitations.
- Real attributed quotes with actual approval; not invented spokesperson language or unsupported expert testimony.
- Clear boilerplate, contact name/role and authorized contact information; approved media assets and links.
- Rights/release permissions for featured people and logos. Avoid marketing superlatives, boastful "industry leading," fake firsts, promotional puff, speculative statistics and misleading charts.
- Useful background/FAQ and ability to interview an informed person (only if genuinely available).

Draft the **press release**, **journalist-specific pitch** (why *their* audience should care), **press kit/FAQ**, and **owned/social announcement** as separate artifacts. A release and a pitch aren't the same piece of writing.

## 4. Editorial journalist outreach

Build a small relevant media/creator list only from verifiable public or authorized professional contact sources. Record **publication, actual beat, geography, recent relevant article/date, audience relevance, story angle, verified contact/route, permission and outreach status**. Don't invent email addresses or scrape personal information without authorization. Avoid pitch blasts.

Personalize with a concrete reason the story matters to their readership. Give useful data, an available expert, interview access, legitimate images, embargo terms **only when agreed**, and a low-effort way to verify facts. If no fit, don't pitch. Follow up courteously and sparingly; stop when declined or requested. Don't impersonate readers, editors, customers or journalists.

The 2026 Cision State of the Media survey (nearly 2,000 journalists, 19 markets; vendor-reported) emphasizes **relevance, original data, experts and accuracy**; journalists said many pitches do not suit their beat. This is directional evidence, not a universal response-rate forecast.

## 5. Distribution: owned newsroom, direct pitch, wire and paid placement

| Route | What you get | Use when | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owned newsroom/blog** | Source-of-truth announcement, stable link, useful assets | Genuine company updates; fact-checking and sharing | Needs audience/distribution |
| **Targeted reporter pitch** | Chance of independent story/interview | Specific beat and news fit, genuine expertise | No guarantee, outreach time; editorial independence |
| **PR wire (PR Newswire/Business Wire etc.)** | Paid geographic/topic release dissemination; possible syndication | Material announcement warranting wide distribution, regulatory or stakeholder requirements | Fees vary; pickup ≠ journalist coverage; links/SEO benefits not guaranteed |
| **Paid advertorial/native media** | Purchased placement | Audience fit and clear commercial disclosure | Must be labeled sponsored/advertising; **not earned editorial** |
| **Podcast/blog/expert collaboration** | Discussion or co-created content | A true expert/customer benefit, strong audience fit | Editorial independence or sponsor terms must be clear |

For paid newswire distribution, **verify current vendor availability, editorial rules, pricing, rights, country, company verification and reporting before purchasing**. There is no reliable fixed price; ask for a quote instead of inventing costs. A release should go to the right audience, not every possible circuit.

**SEO caveat:** syndicated release links and mass posting are not an automatic "backlink strategy." Respect search engine spam rules and mark advertorial/paid links appropriately. Earn durable coverage because a source is useful and trustworthy.

## 6. Distribution pack and approval

A complete pack:
1. **Newsworthiness brief** (who cares, external impact, evidence).
2. **Source register** (dates, facts, claim IDs, approved contacts and quotes).
3. **Main release** (factual, inverted pyramid).
4. **Pitch variants** (local/industry/consumer/technology, only where actual fit).
5. **Media kit** (fact sheet, high-res authorized images, real experts, FAQ, brand marks).
6. **Distribution decision** (owned, targeted pitch, wire, paid; price/coverage hypothesis).
7. **Timeline and approvals** (embargo/exclusive if relevant; legal and company approval).
8. **Measurement** (coverage quality, article relevance, audience understanding, qualified attention, leads and outcomes).

Do not send pitches, book wire services, pay for distribution, publish releases or represent a person as a spokesperson without explicit authorization.

## 7. Measurement and reputation

Track published **independent reporting vs syndicated company copy**, subject relevance, accuracy, source inclusion, interview quality, brand/message recall, qualified visits and real business/reputational outcomes. **No AVE** as a proxy for value, no misleading "potential impressions" sum and no guarantee that coverage caused a sale. Use [AMEC's Integrated Evaluation Framework](https://amecorg.com/amecframework/).

Have a correction/escalation protocol: if a claim is wrong, revise owned release, inform distribution partners as needed, respect corrections and contact internal legal/comms leads for sensitive issues. Do not try to suppress accurate negative coverage or game newsroom results.
