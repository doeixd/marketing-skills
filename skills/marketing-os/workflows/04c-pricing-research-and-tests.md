# Workflow 4C — Pricing: research → price and packaging options → honest tests → decision

**Trigger:** "What should I charge?", raising or lowering prices, discount strategy, tiers/packaging/bundles, a pricing page, subscription pricing, willingness-to-pay research, or a suspected price objection.

**Place:** Optional specialist in [Stage 4](04-offer-and-conversion.md). Value and price perception are in the [product value reference](../references/product-value-and-pricing.md); promotions in [5J](05j-shopify-ecommerce-promotions.md). Pricing decisions belong to the business owner; this workflow supplies evidence and options.

## 1. Set the floor, the ceiling and the goal

- **Floor:** unit cost, fees, shipping, returns, support and acquisition cost → minimum viable price and contribution margin at each candidate price.
- **Ceiling:** the buyer's real alternatives and what they pay for them (including doing nothing).
- **Goal:** profit, growth, positioning, cash, or clearing stock. They lead to different prices.

Missing costs → `[NEEDS INPUT]`; do not recommend a price without a contribution calculation.

## 2. Diagnose whether price is really the problem

A "too expensive" objection often means unclear value, missing proof, surprise costs at checkout (shipping, tax, fees), the wrong audience, or no reference point. Check the page and funnel ([4A CRO](04a-conversion-rate-optimization.md)) and buyer language before touching the number.

## 3. Research willingness to pay

Choose by stakes and sample available. All survey methods overstate what people will actually pay; treat results as ranges and `inferred`.

| Method | What it gives | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Customer and lost-buyer interviews | Reference prices, alternatives, what "worth it" means | 5–15 conversations |
| Van Westendorp price sensitivity (too cheap / bargain / getting expensive / too expensive) | An acceptable price range | ~100+ relevant respondents |
| Gabor-Granger (would you buy at X?) | A rough demand curve for one product | ~100+ respondents |
| Conjoint / MaxDiff | Value of features and tiers relative to price | Larger sample, specialist tooling |
| Own sales history, past price changes, promotions | Real behavior | Clean order data |
| Competitor and substitute price map | Reference points and gaps | Public prices, dated |

## 4. Design price and packaging options

Consider the **structure** before the number: what is included, tiers (each tier must fit a real buyer segment), bundles, quantity breaks, subscription vs one-time, shipping thresholds, guarantees, payment terms. Present the price with honest reference points (cost per use, comparison with the real alternative). A decoy tier or "was" price must be genuine: reference prices need a real, recent selling history (FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing; the EU requires the lowest price of the prior 30 days for announced reductions).

## 5. Test honestly

- Prefer tests that don't show different prices to different people for the same thing at the same time: **sequential** price changes with a comparable period, **geo or market** tests, **new-customer offer or packaging** variants, or pricing a **new** tier or bundle. Simultaneous split price tests risk buyer anger, refunds and, in some jurisdictions or sectors, legal exposure; if run, honor the lower price on request and get owner and legal sign-off.
- Judge by **contribution profit per visitor** (conversion × margin at that price) and downstream effects (returns, refunds, churn, support, repeat rate), never conversion rate alone.
- Fix the duration and decision rule in advance ([experiment methods](../references/testing-and-learning.md)).

## 6. Decide and roll out

Give the owner the options with contribution math, evidence strength and risks. For an increase: decide how existing customers and subscribers are treated, give clear notice, and update every surface (site, feeds, ads, emails, marketplaces) at once so prices never contradict. Subscriptions need clear pre-purchase disclosure of price and renewal terms, consent, and simple cancellation (US ROSCA, Section 5 of the FTC Act and state automatic-renewal laws, many of them strict. The FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated on procedural grounds by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025, and no replacement rule was found as of November 2026).

**Stop conditions:** unknown costs; a proposed reference price with no real selling history; a test design the owner or counsel has not approved.

**Sources:** [FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR 233](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-233), [EU Price Indication Directive guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/unfair-commercial-practices-and-price-indication_en), [FTC Negative Option Rule, 16 CFR 425](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-425).
