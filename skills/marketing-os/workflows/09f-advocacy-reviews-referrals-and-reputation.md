# Workflow 9F — Advocacy and reputation: reviews, referrals, loyalty, and responding when things go wrong

**Trigger:** Getting more reviews or testimonials, a referral or refer-a-friend program, a loyalty or VIP program, user-generated content, negative reviews or comments, a complaint going public, or a reputation or crisis situation.

**Place:** Optional specialist in [Stage 9](09-retention-and-scale.md). Lifecycle messaging mechanics are in [9A](09a-shopify-lifecycle-retention.md)–[9D](09d-cross-channel-lifecycle-and-segmentation.md); creator and affiliate partnerships in [5E](05e-creators-influencers-and-collaborations.md). Load the [reviews, referrals, loyalty and reputation reference](../references/reviews-referrals-loyalty-and-reputation.md). Output: an [advocacy and reputation plan](../templates/advocacy-and-reputation-plan.md).

## 1. Check that advocacy is deserved

Look at return and refund rates, support themes, delivery performance and existing review content. If the experience is the problem, fix it first; asking unhappy customers to advocate amplifies the problem. Route product and service defects to [commercial diagnosis](01-commercial-diagnosis.md).

## 2. Reviews and testimonials

- **Ask everyone**, at a moment after the customer has actually used the product or service, through a channel they consented to. One request and one reminder is usually enough.
- **Never:** write or buy fake reviews, use AI-generated reviews, gate (ask only happy customers), pay or reward for a *positive* review, have staff or relatives review without disclosure, or hide and suppress negative reviews. These breach the FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule and platform policies.
- **Incentives:** where a platform allows them, the reward must not depend on the review being positive and must be disclosed on the review. Google and Amazon prohibit incentives for reviews (Amazon's own Vine program aside).
- **Display** all genuine reviews, including critical ones; show the count, source and date. Get written permission before using a review, name or photo in ads.
- **Respond** to reviews, especially negative ones, using the response guide in the reference.

## 3. Referral program

Design: who can refer, the reward for referrer and friend (double-sided usually works best), when the reward is earned (after the friend's order is past the return window), limits, and how it is tracked. **Economics:** total reward cost per acquired customer must sit under the allowable CAC, and count only *incremental* customers: some referred buyers would have come anyway. **Controls:** self-referral and coupon-site abuse, stacking rules, clear terms. **Disclosure:** a referral reward is a material connection; referrers posting publicly should say they get a reward. Promote it at moments of satisfaction (after delivery, after a good review, in account pages), not at checkout only.

## 4. Loyalty or VIP program

Launch one only if repeat purchase is natural for the category ([Workflow 9](09-retention-and-scale.md)). Choose the simplest mechanism that fits: points, tiers, paid membership, or non-monetary perks (early access, service). The main risk is paying for purchases that would have happened anyway, so measure against a holdout and include the reward liability and breakage in the margin math. Terms must state earning, expiry and changes; expiring earned value without notice destroys trust and can be unlawful.

## 5. User-generated content

Use customers' photos, videos and posts only with explicit permission that covers the specific use (organic repost vs paid ad are different rights). Credit as agreed; never alter content so it misrepresents the product.

## 6. Monitor and respond

Set up monitoring for reviews (site, Google, marketplaces, industry sites), ad and social comments, brand mentions and support channels, with an owner and response-time target. Classify each issue with the severity matrix in the reference: routine (reply within the normal window), elevated (pattern or a customer at risk: fix cause, reply personally), or crisis.

**Crisis protocol:** pause scheduled posts and affected ads; establish facts before speaking; one named spokesperson; a short holding statement that acknowledges, says what is being done and when the next update comes; contact affected customers directly before public statements where possible; legal review for safety, privacy or regulatory matters; never delete legitimate criticism, argue in public, or blame customers; afterwards, publish the fix and hold a post-mortem.

## 7. Measure

Review volume, rating, recency and response rate; share of orders reviewed; referral share of new customers, cost per referred customer and their retention; loyalty members' incremental repeat rate vs holdout; issue volume and time to resolution. Feed review and complaint themes back into [buyer research](02-buyer-discovery.md) and the context file as `buyer-reported`.

**Stop conditions:** a request to create, buy, gate or suppress reviews (decline and explain the permitted route); reward economics above allowable CAC; an unresolved product-safety or legal issue (escalate to the owner and counsel before any public message).
