# Workflow 9G — Subscription retention: diagnose churn → fix causes → save honestly → win back

**Trigger:** Subscription, membership, SaaS or replenishment business with churn concerns; "reduce churn", cancellation flow, failed payments or dunning, pause/skip options, trial-to-paid, renewal reminders, win-back campaigns, or net revenue retention.

**Place:** Optional specialist in [Stage 9](09-retention-and-scale.md). Cohort methods are in [8C](08c-customer-segmentation-rfm-and-clv.md); lifecycle messaging in [9D](09d-cross-channel-lifecycle-and-segmentation.md); SaaS activation in [5O](05o-b2b-saas-demand-generation.md); pricing and renewal terms in [4C](04c-pricing-research-and-tests.md).

## 1. Measure churn properly

Define the metrics once in the [KPI dictionary](../templates/marketing-measurement-and-kpi-dictionary.md): customer (logo) churn, revenue churn, net revenue retention, and retention **curves by signup cohort** (monthly averages hide when people leave). Split by plan, acquisition source, offer used at signup, and tenure. Separate **voluntary** churn (chose to cancel) from **involuntary** churn (payment failed).

**Deliverable:** cohort retention table and the two or three points where most subscribers are lost.

## 2. Find the causes

Combine cancellation reasons (one required question, free text allowed), support contacts before cancelling, usage or order patterns of churned vs retained customers, and interviews with recent cancellers. Common causes: never reached first value; too much product arriving; price vs perceived value; a discounted first period ending; a better alternative; the need ended; service failure. Customers acquired with deep discounts or through misleading ads churn fast: check churn by acquisition source before blaming retention.

## 3. Fix involuntary churn first (usually the cheapest win)

Pre-expiry card reminders, the payment provider's automatic card updater and smart retries, a clear failed-payment sequence by email/SMS/in-app with a one-click update link, a grace period before cancelling access, and alternative payment methods.

## 4. Improve the experience that causes voluntary churn

- **Onboarding to first value:** the first delivery or session sets expectations; guide the customer to the outcome they signed up for.
- **Flexibility:** skip, pause, change frequency, swap product, downgrade. A paused subscriber is worth more than a cancelled one.
- **Advance notice:** renewal and charge reminders, especially for annual plans and after trials or intro prices. They reduce refunds, chargebacks and resentment, and many laws require them.
- **Ongoing value:** usage summaries, what's new, member benefits that are real.

## 5. Design an honest cancellation flow

Cancelling must be as easy as signing up, through the same medium, without calls or repeated screens designed to obstruct. Within that: ask the reason once; offer **one** relevant alternative matched to the reason (pause, lower frequency, cheaper plan, help with the product); accept "no" immediately; confirm in writing with the end date. No guilt-tripping, hidden buttons or forced chats: they produce chargebacks, complaints and legal risk (US ROSCA, state automatic-renewal laws such as California's, EU/UK consumer law). Test save offers against a holdout: many "saved" customers leave a month later.

## 6. Win back, sparingly

Segment former subscribers by reason and tenure. Contact only those with permission, after a sensible gap, with what has actually changed or a relevant plan; exclude people who left over a service failure until it is fixed. Measure reactivation and second-period retention, not just reactivations.

## 7. Review

Track retention curves by cohort after each change, involuntary recovery rate, save-offer incremental retention, refund and chargeback rates, and CAC payback and CLV by acquisition source. Feed churn-by-source findings back to the [media plan](05p-media-plan-and-budget-allocation.md): cheap customers who leave are not cheap.

**Stop conditions:** a request to make cancellation harder or to hide renewal terms (decline; propose flexibility and value instead); churn driven by a product or fulfilment failure that marketing cannot fix.

**Sources:** [FTC Negative Option Rule, 16 CFR 425](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-425), [California Automatic Renewal Law](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&division=7.&title=&part=3.&chapter=1.&article=9.), [Stripe: revenue recovery and failed-payment retries](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery).
