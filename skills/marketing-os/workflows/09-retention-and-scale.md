# Workflow 9 — Retention, merchandising, and profitable growth

**Trigger:** Product acquisition is working, existing customer base exists, repeat purchase is possible, or the store is looking for more profitable growth than increasing spend alone.

**Promotion extension:** Community participation, real customer advocacy, ethical referral/affiliate collaboration and earned sharing can complement retention. Use [Promotion 5C](05c-promotion-strategy-and-distribution.md) and [Creators 5E](05e-creators-influencers-and-collaborations.md) for partner terms/permissions. Never buy fake reviews or imply repeat purchasing is naturally frequent for every category; evaluate qualified lifetime value and retention before scaling.

## A. Understand whether repeat purchase is realistic

The product category determines natural repeat behavior. A long-lived keepsake may be purchased infrequently; gifts can recur by occasion; consumables may invite replenishment. Do not assume a subscription or monthly reactivation is appropriate. Inspect actual return time, order frequencies, product adjacency, repeat contribution and customer service outcomes.

## B. Find the next job in the customer relationship

| Customer moment | Question | Legitimate intervention | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediately after purchase | Are expectations clear? | Accurate confirmation, care/usage guidance | Don't flood customers with promotion |
| Delivery and first use | Did promise match reality? | Support, truthful setup info, feedback invitation | Resolve actual product defects |
| Later related need | Is there an adjacent product or occasion? | Relevant product education, compatible cross-sell | Avoid irrelevant offers and forced scarcity |
| Repeatable need | Is there a natural replacement/replenishment cadence? | Consent-based reminder or subscription option if real | No arbitrary frequency or traps |
| Customer advocacy | Was experience genuinely positive? | Optional review/referral invitation | Don't solicit invented or incentivized-undisclosed endorsements |
| Lapsed customer | Is the offering still relevant? | Low-frequency, permission-based communication | Respect opt-out and privacy |

## C. Extend the brand without losing clarity

Use category entry points, product adjacency, and customer demand to propose complementary SKUs, bundles or new occasions. Maintain recognizable brand assets, category legitimacy and product truth. Evaluate broad reach and physical availability when considering more ad spend or channels. Do not overfit the brand to a single narrow persona or over-exclude future category buyers.

## D. Profitability and causal checks

Track net contribution by customer cohort after refunds, variable support/fulfillment costs and acquisition expense where available. Distinguish attributed repeat orders from **incremental** purchases caused by lifecycle messages. A good growth decision can be better retention, a better product page, higher compatible basket value, lower returns, greater mental availability, or additional profitable reach—not always new ads.

## E. Decision gate and handoff

If customers are dissatisfied or return rates are high, focus on experience/product before retention marketing. If repeat cycles don't naturally exist, prioritize referrals, new use occasions or broader acquisition rather than pushing repeated emails. If profitable cohorts and operational capacity support growth, plan a constrained expansion and measure it.

**Handoff:** additional buying occasions, returned buyer language, product and service insights → [buyer discovery](02-buyer-discovery.md), [positioning](03-positioning-and-brand.md), [channel strategy](05-channel-and-campaign.md) and [commercial diagnosis](01-commercial-diagnosis.md).
