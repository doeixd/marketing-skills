# Worked strategic diagnosis — a hypothetical vintage-inspired ring collection

**All data and customer motivations are illustrative; there is no actual brand, product feed or performance history in this example.** Do not describe these as research findings or make unverified claims in live advertising.

## Request

"Our Facebook ads get clicks but not enough orders. Write new primary text and headlines for a vintage-inspired ring collection."

## Step 1: stop and identify the real problem

Possible causes, **not observed facts**:

| Hypothesis | Why it might matter | Evidence to request | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creative attracts curiosity, not buying interest | Aesthetic imagery may drive unqualified clicks | Spend/click/purchase outcomes by creative | Test a more product-specific angle |
| Fit anxiety | Rings require correct sizing | Size-chart visibility, support questions, return reasons | Fix size guidance and photographs |
| Value unclear at displayed price | Customer lacks a reason to choose this vs alternative | Prices, materials, comparison details, reviews | Explain verified attributes; re-evaluate pricing |
| Delivery friction | Gift buyer needs a date | Delivery estimator, actual fulfillment data | Clarify or improve shipping; do not invent dates |
| Brand unfamiliar | Some buyers distrust an unknown seller | Genuine reviews, source of traffic, repeated brand cues | Improve credibility/branding |
| Measurement discrepancy | Sales may not be accurately attributed | Pixel/CAPI, checkout data, attribution windows | Audit data before inferring copy failure |

No conclusion is warranted until evidence is available. Do not proclaim a winning copy formula.

## Step 2: market and positioning (hypotheses)

- **Category:** ring accessories / vintage-inspired jewelry (the precise category depends on real catalog).
- **Alternatives:** minimalist ring, genuine vintage ring, fashion-jewelry marketplace listing, other gifts, nonpurchase.
- **Points of parity:** legitimate product listing, size choice, price transparency, material information, actual fulfillment, clear photos.
- **Potential point of difference:** design details, *if* inspection confirms they are distinctive relative to relevant alternatives.
- **Best-fit buyer circumstances:** stylistic self-selection, gift-buying occasion, exploration of decorative jewelry.
- **Trade-off:** an elaborate design can appeal to some buyers and be undesirable for minimalist shoppers. This is not necessarily a flaw to conceal.

## Step 3: buyer/recipient and decision process

| Buying situation | Job | Pull | Anxiety | Inertia | Evidence to gather |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-purchase | Find a visually expressive accessory | Design and fit with existing style | Product quality and physical fit | Keep current rings | Reviews, size chart, customer language |
| Gift | Find a thoughtful, suitable present | Personal-looking design | Recipient taste, size, arrival | Gift card or familiar retailer | Gift reviews, exchange policy, delivery |
| Browsing | Discover choices worth considering | Variety, selection and visuals | Which style/size to choose | Continue searching elsewhere | Search terms, collection usability |

## Step 4: brand memory and signaling

- **Brand name:** unknown; never invent one.
- **Distinctive assets:** unknown; inspect actual supplied logo, typography, product photography and packaging.
- **Potential CEPs:** birthday gift, style refresh, specific occasion—each needs confirmation of relevance to this seller.
- **Quality signals:** only actual photos, materials, measurements, policy and real customer accounts.
- **Design constraint:** different ads can express different motivations, but should continue to show the same real brand identifiers.

## Step 5: prioritize interventions

1. **Blocker check:** if actual product sizing/material/returns are missing, fix the listing before running reassurance copy.
2. **Positioning test:** contrast self-expression with gifting and product-led discovery; use source-grounded copy and creative.
3. **Brand recognition:** keep real name/logo/visual cues visible and consistent where appropriate.
4. **Measurement:** compare purchases or contribution/qualified conversions, not CTR alone; avoid causal claims if creative/offer/audience differ.

## Step 6: draft only truthful, modestly worded concepts

### Concept A — self-expression

- **Primary text:** Explore vintage-inspired rings and find a design that fits your sense of style.
- **Headline:** Find Your Ring Style
- **Description:** Browse the collection
- **Creative:** real ring in a legible close-up with real brand cue.
- **Hypothesis:** style-message relevance may improve qualified purchases versus product-led copy.

### Concept B — gifting

- **Primary text:** Looking for a gift with a little personality? Explore vintage-inspired ring designs.
- **Headline:** A Thoughtful Ring Gift
- **Description:** Explore styles
- **Creative:** actual product and, only if verified, any supplied gift packaging.
- **Hypothesis:** an occasion-oriented concept may work better for gift-shopping situations.

### Concept C — product discovery

- **Primary text:** Take a closer look at vintage-inspired ring styles. Compare designs and find a favorite.
- **Headline:** Explore Ring Designs
- **Description:** See the collection
- **Creative:** clear montage of actual stocked styles.
- **Hypothesis:** clear product discovery may increase purchase quality relative to emotion-focused creative.

These are drafts only. Actual brand name, price, materials, SKU-specific variants and shipping details must be checked before launch. Brand cue is a creative direction, not fabricated inventory.

## Step 7: what would change the diagnosis?

If verified conversion data shows sufficient purchase volume and strong returns on an existing message, do not replace that message just to seem creative. If high landing-page bounce coincides with broken mobile UX, prioritize the page. If customers report material mismatch, fix the claim or product. If a gift segment purchases more but also returns more, evaluate net contribution rather than gross purchase volume.

The point is **better marketing decisions, not merely more ad variations**.
