# Worked example: discover a jewelry brand and build a messaging library

**Illustrative scenario only:** A fictional label sells *vintage-inspired patterned rings* online. We have a product photo and a listing confirming only the design category. We do **not** have verified materials, manufacturing location, founder history, shipment timing, reviews, stock claims or established audience research.

## A. Evidence before interpretation

| ID | What we know | Status | Allowed |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Listing describes a vintage-inspired ring | verified within hypothetical prompt | "Vintage-inspired" |
| F2 | Photo visibly shows a patterned design | observed within hypothetical prompt | "Patterned details" |
| H1 | Some buyers value expressive design | hypothesis | Explore as a message angle, not a consumer statistic |
| H2 | Some buyers look for a thoughtful gift | hypothesis | Try as a situation, not a reported buyer motive |
| U1 | Materials, fabrication, guarantees, shipping | unknown | Don't mention as claims |

An actual agent would link the product page/photo, research date, owner input and customer review references.

## B. Claimed, expressed and perceived identity

| Claimed | Expressed | Perceived |
|---|---|---|
| No owner statement provided | Listing/photo show patterned, vintage-inspired aesthetics | Unknown until real reviews/interviews |

**Consequent decision:** Do not invent a founding story, "heirloom-quality" claim, or permanent personality. Explore a *provisional* brand voice emphasizing observational detail and a modest, expressive tone.

## C. Brand positioning candidates

1. **Design-led:** For people choosing jewelry by distinctive aesthetic, the ring offers detailed vintage-inspired styling. Supported by F1/F2; buyers' preference is a hypothesis.
2. **Gift-led:** In a gift search, the ring may be an option when a personal visual style matters. The gifting motivation remains H2.
3. **Collection-led:** A catalog of visibly different patterns could support browsing and comparing styles, *if* a real collection exists; not used yet.

Positioning against specific competitors requires real alternative products and customer evidence.

## D. Provisional voice (not discovered voice)

- **Style:** conversational but restrained; image-led and specific, not breathless.
- **Word choices:** ring, patterned, vintage-inspired, details, design.
- **Sentence patterns:** short description plus invitation; occasional fragment.
- **Avoid:** luxury, handcrafted, hypoallergenic, heirloom, timeless quality, limited stock and "perfect for everyone" unless individually documented.
- **Brand assets:** unknown. A real ad needs the label name/logo and approved visuals before launch.

**Say / don't say:**

| Prefer | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "A vintage-inspired ring with patterned detail." | "The most luxurious handcrafted ring." | Only the former is based on available evidence |
| "Explore the design." | "Last chance to get yours." | No actual scarcity evidence |
| "Find a style that feels like yours." | "Everyone will love it." | Preference is personal; universal outcome unsupported |

## E. Copy distillation

**Candidate one-liner:** "Vintage-inspired rings with details worth a closer look." (Use brand-specific plural only if the collection is verified.)

**Selling-point bullets:**
- **Vintage-inspired design** — recognizable design direction. (F1)
- **Patterned details** — a visible design characteristic. (F2)
- **Explore the look** — an invitation, not a material or quality claim. (F1/F2)

**Headline bank:**
- "Explore Vintage-Inspired Rings"
- "Look Closer at the Details"
- "Find a Ring with Character"

**Supporting description candidates:**
- "See the design"
- "Explore the collection" *(only if multiple actual products exist)*
- "View ring details"

**Meta concept A — Aesthetic / self-expression**

- Primary: "Vintage-inspired detailing, with a little character in every pattern. Take a closer look."
- Headline: "A Ring with Character"
- Description: "Explore the details"
- Visual: real macro of the ring, authentic scale and color, visible brand cue.
- Hypothesis: an aesthetic message resonates with buyers browsing for style. Not proven.

**Meta concept B — Gift consideration**

- Primary: "Picking out a ring for someone? Start with the details that feel like their style."
- Headline: "Explore Vintage-Inspired Rings"
- Description: "View ring styles" *(only if catalog supports plural)*
- Visual: real product and packaging only if packaging is available and authentic.
- Hypothesis: gift shoppers may respond to personal style rather than a generic "perfect gift" claim.

**Meta concept C — Product-forward**

- Primary: "A closer look at patterned, vintage-inspired design. Explore the ring and its details."
- Headline: "Look Closer"
- Description: "See the design"
- Visual: macro and side views from real photography; no invented material features.
- Hypothesis: product-forward precision may increase qualified consideration.

## F. What the agent should ask or research next

Priority: brand name and assets, true collection size, verified materials, actual customer feedback, return/sizing/shipping policy, realistic competitors, existing copy samples and which voice treatment the owner approves.

**Handoff:** These are candidate messages only. Convert them into approved brand copy after brand identity, product proof and buyer understanding improve.
