# Worked example: hypothetical vintage-inspired ring collection

**Demonstration only.** There is no actual brand, catalog, sales history or buyer research in this example. Motivations are plausible hypotheses, not reported customer insights. Real campaign use requires SKU-level details, image review and policy/offer verification.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | What is known? | Status | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | The prompt describes a vintage-inspired ring collection | hypothesis | Use category language, avoid material assumptions |
| E2 | Photos may show ornate shapes | unknown until inspected | Obtain close-up photos before claiming specific design details |
| E3 | Shipping and returns terms | unknown | Do not imply a delivery deadline or easy returns |
| E4 | Price, materials, sizes, stock and discounts | unknown | Do not manufacture claims or urgency |

## Buyer-decision map (hypotheses)

### Situation A — personal style

- Trigger: looking to refresh a jewelry collection or find a distinctive look.
- Job: select a ring that expresses individual taste.
- Pull: design, aesthetics, style-fit.
- Anxiety: will it look like the image? Does it fit? Is material accurate?
- Habit: keep wearing existing rings.
- Alternatives: simple contemporary band, thrifted vintage piece, no purchase.
- Proof to request: actual photo and dimension for each SKU, material details, size chart.

### Situation B — gifting

- Trigger: upcoming celebration or desire for a thoughtful gift.
- Job: give an accessory that feels personally chosen.
- Pull: distinct look, emotional significance.
- Anxiety: will recipient like it, will size fit, will it arrive?
- Habit: gift card or another familiar gift.
- Alternatives: necklaces, other rings, gift cards, no purchase.
- Proof to request: gift packaging, actual return/exchange terms, delivery estimator.

### Situation C — exploration

- Trigger: casually browsing ring designs.
- Job: compare and discover attractive choices.
- Pull: assortment, visible shapes and details.
- Anxiety: how do I tell styles/sizes apart?
- Habit: continue browsing elsewhere.
- Alternatives: competitive catalogs, marketplaces.
- Proof to request: clear collection page, sorting/filters, product photography.

## Feature-to-benefit ladder

`Visible vintage-inspired design (confirm actual item)` → `distinctive look` → `choice beyond plain bands` → `possibility of self-expression`.

The physical design is potentially verifiable; the self-expression motive remains a hypothesis until buyers say so or tests give supportive evidence.

## Three distinct ad concepts

### A — personal style

- **Primary text:** Vintage-inspired rings with character in every curve. Find a style that feels like yours.
- **Headline:** Explore Vintage-Style Rings
- **Description:** Find your favorite
- **CTA:** Shop Now (check account availability)
- **Creative:** detailed product close-up of the actual SKU.
- **Hypothesis:** style-led positioning improves cost per purchase against product-detail-led positioning in a comparable test.

### B — gifting

- **Primary text:** Some gifts feel more personal than others. Explore rings with vintage-inspired details for someone special.
- **Headline:** A Gift Worth Choosing
- **Description:** Browse the collection
- **CTA:** Shop Now
- **Creative:** actual jewelry and, if supplied, verified packaging — don't depict nonexistent gift wrapping.
- **Hypothesis:** gifting relevance improves qualified purchase rate during genuine gift-shopping periods.

### C — product exploration

- **Primary text:** Explore vintage-inspired ring designs, compare the details, and find the one that catches your eye.
- **Headline:** Find Your Next Ring
- **Description:** See all ring styles
- **CTA:** Shop Now
- **Creative:** multiple real SKUs or simple product montage from available inventory.
- **Hypothesis:** collection exploration suits browsing audiences more than a single emotional hook.

## Tradeoffs and blockers

No ad asserts materials, care instructions, production process, shipping, inventory or discount. Actual sizing and returns policy are unknown. An agent should recommend a size chart and clear product detail photos; they cannot be conjured by copy.

## Preflight

Check the ad/page/offer together; verify actual products and size options. Review the platform preview on the selected placement. If conducting an experiment, keep the image, audience, price and landing page as comparable as possible when isolating copy. Evaluate purchases/CPA or an appropriate goal metric, not just CTR.
