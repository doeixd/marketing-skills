# Worked example — from buyer understanding to ad test and sales decision

**Entirely hypothetical example.** No actual company, customer interviews, ad-account data, profit figures or results are claimed. Illustrates the chain of reasoning and *what each answer changes*.

## Product context

Product: a vintage-inspired ring collection. Assume real photos and descriptions exist; don't assume specific materials, warranty, discounts, delivery or gift packaging. Objective: acquire **profitable** first-time orders while preserving brand recognition. Unknowns are explicitly marked.

| Stage | Important question | Possible finding | Decision / action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Economics | How much contribution is available for paid acquisition? | Illustration: $50 net order, $30 variable cost, $20 pre-ad contribution | Break-even $20 CPA; seek lower CPA to leave operating profit, pending validated costs |
| 2. Buyer research | Why might someone consider these rings? | **Hypotheses:** self-expression, gift buying, design discovery | Explore review/support evidence; distinguish buyer vs recipient |
| 3. Positioning | What makes it attractive relative to alternatives? | Actual catalog shows ornamental, vintage-inspired design | Use aesthetic character as value; do not invent craftsmanship/durability |
| 4. Objections | What could cause regret? | **Hypotheses:** size, perceived material quality, recipient taste | Inspect product info, actual returns/size policies and customer questions |
| 5. Offer/PDP | Can the buyer verify the promise and buy? | Product photos exist, sizing information still unknown | Audit sizing, variants and total price before promotion |
| 6. Channel | What commercial job should ads serve? | Product discovery/qualified conversion | Meta product-led concepts; optionally brand-memory assets using real identifiers |
| 7. Creative | Which competing hypotheses should we test? | Aesthetic vs gifting vs design-selection | Distinct creatives and complete ad units, each with own buying occasion |
| 8. Launch | Is everything approved and accurately measured? | Not yet — hypothetical | Verify policies, placement previews, purchase event and actual budget approval |
| 9. Learn | What should we do if clicks don't become sales? | Unknown until launched | Inspect traffic quality, price/fit concerns, checkout and tracking; change most evidenced constraint |
| 10. Lifecycle | Are ring buyers likely to purchase again? | Unknown | Assess gifting occasions or compatible products; don't assume subscriptions |

## The three creative briefs

### A — Self-expression

- Buyer trigger: wanting a distinctive accessory.
- Value hypothesis: visual details that reflect personal style.
- Proof: accurate imagery of actual designs.
- Primary text: "Vintage-inspired ring designs with details worth exploring. Find a style that speaks to you."
- Headline: "Explore Vintage-Style Rings"
- Description: "See the collection"
- Visual: close-up of the available ring(s), real brand cue.
- Landing page: actual collection/PDP that shows the designs and sizing.
- Test insight sought: does design-led communication produce more profitable orders than a product-only baseline?

### B — Gifting

- Buyer trigger: looking for a personal gift.
- Value hypothesis: a carefully chosen design communicates thoughtfulness.
- Proof: genuine design imagery; actual delivery/exchange information only when verified.
- Primary text: "Looking for a gift with personality? Browse vintage-inspired rings and find a design worth sharing."
- Headline: "Find a Thoughtful Gift"
- Description: "Browse ring styles"
- Visual: real product and brand, not fabricated packaging.
- Landing page: accessible size choices, real shipping/returns information.
- Test insight sought: does a gift occasion create stronger purchase intent or merely more curiosity?

### C — Product discovery

- Buyer trigger: comparing different ring designs.
- Value hypothesis: visible details and selection help evaluate styles.
- Proof: actual assortment.
- Primary text: "A closer look makes all the difference. Explore vintage-inspired ring designs and compare the details."
- Headline: "See the Ring Designs"
- Description: "Explore available styles"
- Visual: accurate side-by-side shots of stocked variants with real brand cue.
- Landing page: collection with product availability and specs.
- Test insight sought: does product clarity improve qualified traffic?

## Stop and pivot examples

- If people click but can't choose a size, **fix selection and measurements**, not another hook.
- If gift-oriented creative produces sales but more returns, compare **net contribution** and recipient-fit problems before declaring a winner.
- If Meta reports excellent ROAS while overall sales barely change, review attribution and consider causal measurement when volume permits.
- If aesthetic ads become distinctive to viewers but nobody remembers the brand, improve brand cue integration, not merely copy length.
- If true variable costs leave only $10 contribution, revise the allowable CPA and campaign economics; the illustrative $20 threshold no longer applies.

The end product is not 'three nice ads'; it is **three grounded market hypotheses, a real offer, a valid purchase path, a measurement plan and a decision process**.
